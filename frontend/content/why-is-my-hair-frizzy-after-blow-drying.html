Notice when the frizz starts. If a section puffs up as you dry it, try a smaller section and direct the nozzle along it. If strands scatter as you pass, check whether the airflow and attachment give you enough control. If the finish looks smooth at first and changes later, check what happened after drying before repeating the same pass. This page is for a separate hair dryer, used with or without a hand-held brush.

Quick answer

Change one thing that matches the moment frizz appears: a smaller section if it puffs while drying, more control at the ends if they scatter, a diffuser if waves lose definition, and an after-drying check if it changes later. Compare against an unchanged section before keeping the change.

Using a powered hot-air brush instead? That tool has its own starting state and handling, covered in why a hot-air brush leaves hair frizzy.

Find when the frizz starts

Pick the row that matches what you saw, make the one adjustment, and look at the next section before changing anything else.

When it happens What you see One adjustment
A section puffs up while drying The section expands and lifts away from the brush or your hand Take a smaller section and point the nozzle along it, from roots toward ends
Ends scatter during the pass Loose ends fly outward as the air reaches them Fit the concentrator, lower the airflow setting, and hold the ends with the brush or your fingers while the air passes
Waves lose definition under direct air A wave pattern separates into a fuzzy, undefined shape Spread the airflow with a diffuser instead of a concentrator; see how to diffuse wavy hair
The finish changes once dry Smooth at first, then frizz appears later in the day Note when and where it changed; see why a blowout does not last

The first two rows are about control during the pass. The third is a different finish goal, and the fourth begins after you have finished drying, so repeating the same pass rarely helps it.

A short trial with two sections

On your next ordinary blow-dry, pick two working sections on the same side, similar in length and dampness. Dry the first exactly as you normally do. Dry the second with one change only, such as a narrower section or the nozzle pointing down the length. Then compare the two, side by side, once both have cooled.

  1. Write the change you made to the second section.
  2. Note whether the frizz started at the same moment in both.
  3. Keep the change only if the second section looks better to you.

This is an observation you can repeat, not a score or a product test. Product, length and humidity all vary between days, so give one change two or three sessions before deciding it does or does not help.

Check the nozzle and the brush

A concentrator narrows the air so you can aim it along one section. Without it, a wide stream can reach neighbouring hair and lift it while you work. If your dryer came with one, check that it is seated as the manual directs. For the technique, see how to use a concentrator nozzle.

A finishing attachment is a separate step. Dyson’s UK Supersonic Flyaway attachment support page gives its own starting condition and movement for that one part; see how to use a flyaway attachment rather than applying it to every dryer.

If you use a brush, hold the section with enough tension that the ends do not flick away, and move the brush and the air in the same direction. A comb attachment is a different tool with its own method; see how to use a hair dryer comb attachment.
