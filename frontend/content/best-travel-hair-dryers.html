Choose a travel hair dryer by its electrical rating first, then by what packs. A plug adapter changes the plug shape, not the voltage. For international use, choose only a model whose exact manual documents the destination supply, then check the plug and any adapter's power rating.

Automatic voltage and manual selectors are different

“Dual voltage” covers two mechanisms. An automatic wide-range dryer accepts any supply within its published range without a switch. A manual selector dryer needs you to set the correct voltage before plugging in. The exact manual decides which applies; a family name or retailer filter does not. A single-voltage dryer accepts one range only, and a converter is not a universal fix: some makers exclude converters entirely.

Power, airflow and results are separate questions

A rated input in watts says what the dryer draws, not how much air reaches your hair or how long drying takes. Compare each model's documented input power and any published airflow separately, and regard drying speed as unmeasured unless someone has measured it. This guide does not rank travel dryers on speed. The wattage and electricity-use guide explains what the rating label does and does not tell you.

Check the destination separately

Country supply, socket shape, the dryer's rating and the maker's restrictions are separate checks. For example, a trip from the US to Israel calls for a dryer whose manual covers the local supply and a suitable plug adapter; a US-only 120 V dryer does not become compatible through an adapter. Use an authoritative source for the destination supply rather than a general table. The adapter-versus-converter worksheet owns the electrical decision in full.
