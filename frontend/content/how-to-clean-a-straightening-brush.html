A straightening brush is an electrical styling appliance, not an ordinary brush to soak in a bowl. Identify the exact model and its care instructions before removing hair or surface residue between its bristles. A detachable-looking surface or a familiar brush shape does not establish permission to wash it.

Quick answer

Switch off, unplug and let the appliance cool as its instructions require. Remove loose hair using the permitted method, then clean only the surfaces and with the materials the maker allows. Complete any drying requirement before reuse. Cleaning is not a repair for damaged bristles, a loose cable or abnormal operation.

Separate the appliance from nearby brush formats

An airflow brush has air outlets and may have a filter-care task. A dry-hair smoothing brush has its own heated contact surface and bristle arrangement. Use hot-air-brush cleaning for the former rather than inventing an intake-cleaning step for the latter.

For a non-electrical brush, ordinary hair-brush care is another owner. Its washing permissions do not transfer to an electrical handle, heated bristles or a control seam.

Begin with the model's permitted access

Check the full model name and generation before using a support article. Similar names within one brand can describe different surfaces, accessories and care directions. A removable attachment also needs an explicit removal method; do not prise off a part that only appears separate.

Keep a named damp-cloth instruction specific

The UK original ghd glide cleaning article describes an unplugged, cooled appliance, removal of hair and wiping its heat plate and bristle gaps with a damp wipe or cloth. That instruction belongs to the original model covered by the article.

Do not transfer it to glide+, glide+ max, rise or a different brand without checking that appliance's directions. A support article for a related tool is not permission to spray cleaner over yours or rinse the head under a tap.

Follow a controlled external-care sequence

  1. Identify the exact model and read its current maintenance instructions.
  2. Complete shutdown, unplugging and cooling before handling the contact surface.
  3. Support the appliance on a suitable stable area and remove strands that come free through the permitted method.
  4. Check for rough, bent or damaged parts rather than assuming every snag is residue.
  5. Apply only the documented surface-care method, keeping any permitted cloth use within the specified areas.
  6. Complete the required drying and storage steps before the next use.

If a strand or residue remains after permitted care, ask the maker before using a stronger cleaner, sharper point or disassembly. Do not turn resistance into a reason to bend the bristle array.

Check condition before returning to styling

Inspect the bristles, contact area, controls and cord after care. If the tool is damaged or behaves abnormally, use the manufacturer's service route rather than repeatedly powering it to test whether cleaning worked.

For the next styling session, return to how to use a straightening brush. Cleaning instructions do not change the required starting hair state, styling technique or permitted products. Use the current model manual for those decisions.

Check visible hair and residue as the appliance is used and stored. Follow a maker's schedule where supplied; this guide does not assign a weekly soak or a shared cleaning interval to every electrical brush.
