<p>For curly hair, the attachment can matter as much as the dryer itself. A good diffuser, reliable heat settings and manageable airflow make it easier to dry hair in a way that suits your preferred finish.</p>
<h2>Start with the diffuser</h2>
<p>Check whether the diffuser is included, sold separately or compatible with the model you are considering. It should fit securely. A loose attachment is frustrating and makes a long drying routine needlessly awkward.</p>
<p>A diffuser distributes air over a broader area than a concentrator nozzle. That can be useful when you want to dry curls while avoiding a narrow stream of air. Different diffuser shapes feel different in use, so pay attention to bowl depth, prongs and how comfortably the attachment reaches your roots.</p>
<h2>Controls are more useful than hype</h2>
<p>Choose a dryer with at least a few heat and airflow settings. You may want stronger airflow for initial drying and gentler airflow for finishing. The ability to change settings matters more than a single headline wattage figure.</p>
<p>Prepare hair according to your own routine, apply products as directed and work in sections where helpful. Avoid approaching hair drying as a race: controlled airflow and a comfortable grip are more valuable than repeatedly using the hottest setting.</p>
<h2>When a concentrator is useful</h2>
<p>Curly hair does not mean you must always use a diffuser. A concentrator can be useful when you occasionally want a smoother blow-dry or to target roots. A versatile dryer should allow both approaches.</p>

<h2>The bottom line</h2>
<p>The best dryer for curly hair is one with a secure, useful diffuser and enough control to adapt the heat and airflow to your routine.</p>
