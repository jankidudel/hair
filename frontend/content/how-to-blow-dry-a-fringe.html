A fringe is a small working area with a visible direction. Separate it from the surrounding hair before trying to shape it. This method uses a separate hair dryer and a manual styling brush; a heated brush or straightener has different preparation and handling instructions.

Quick answer

Isolate the fringe, choose whether it should fall forward, to one side or outward in two curtain pieces, then guide a manageable section with the brush while the dryer follows along it. Keep the airflow controllable, finish the required drying and let the shape cool before judging it. Avoid repeatedly reworking the whole front when only one piece needs attention.

Make the fringe a separate working section

Bring the hair to the starting condition your dryer and chosen preparation product permit. Remove excess water gently rather than shaping a dripping bundle. Keep the surrounding lengths clear so they cannot join the brush accidentally. A short fringe can be handled early in the routine if it settles before the longer sections are ready.

Look at the intended parting before switching on. A forward fringe and curtain pieces need different release directions. This tutorial does not change the haircut or guarantee that a growth pattern will disappear. For the rest of the head, use the full blow-dry routine.

Use a brush the short section can follow

Choose a manual brush whose instructions allow dryer use and whose curve the working length can reach and release from. A broad barrel that loses the ends is not easier merely because it is larger. Round-brush size separates the published measurements from the actual reach check.

The Dyson UK fringe guide describes a separate dryer with a concentrator and round brush. Confirm the exact compatible nozzle for your own dryer. A brand-level tutorial does not establish fit between different attachment generations.

Guide one piece through a visible path

  1. Separate the working fringe from neighbouring hair and decide its resting direction.
  2. Rehearse placing and withdrawing the brush without the dryer running. Keep the wrap shallow enough to reverse comfortably.
  3. Position the brush under the section without scraping the scalp. Keep the dryer clear of the face and the nozzle off the brush.
  4. Guide the brush along the section while directing moving airflow with it. Use settings you can control within the appliance instructions.
  5. Shape the ends only as far as the intended finish needs, then withdraw the brush along a clear path.
  6. Complete the drying, use a permitted cool-shot finish if useful and let the piece settle before handling it again.

For curtain pieces, work one side at a time and guide the ends outward from the face. The ghd UK curtain tutorial documents that brush-led direction. It does not make an extra curling-tool pass a required part of this routine.

If the brush catches, stop the dryer and undo the last turn gently. Use the caught-brush guide rather than pulling through resistance. No fixed number of turns, seconds or universal heat setting is needed here.

Keep the finish small

Check the front in its resting position. Another product or hot tool is optional and needs its own compatible directions. If you want to hold the fringe aside while getting ready, follow temporary creaseless-clip placement; the clip should not enter the active dryer path.
