Quick answer

Choose ghd Original if you want a simple fixed-temperature styler and its 24 mm plates fit your usual sections. Compare ghd Gold if you want the slightly wider standard plate and ghd’s dual-zone control. Both use a preset. If adjustable temperature is the reason you are replacing your iron, start with the UK adjustable-temperature shortlist.

Check the upgrade against your own sections

With your current tool cool and unplugged, place your usual front section across its plates. Then repeat with a wider section from the back. If both already fit and you can turn the tool for a bend, the extra width may make little difference to your routine. If the front is already crowded, compare the body and plate clearance before stepping up in width.

If you are buying a replacement because the old tool has failed, compare the exact UK package and warranty as well as fit. Read ghd Gold vs Platinum+ for that upgrade, or ghd Original vs Chronos if that is the other model on your shortlist.

If the shortest sections are the reason you are looking for a different plate shape, ghd Mini vs Original compares the narrower UK format with the Original.

Work out what you would gain by changing tools

Start with the front section you take most often. If you can get the plate near that section and turn it comfortably with your current styler, note whether the problem is farther down the hair. The Gold offers a slightly wider plate, which makes sense to consider if the sections you take at the back are close to the edge of the Original’s working surface. The shape around the plates affects how close you can get to a short front section, so check the full head when access is your priority.

Consider the controls separately. Both models use a preset, so changing between them gives you a different ghd control design with the same listed styling temperature. If you want another way of selecting heat, use the adjustable shortlist linked above. If you liked the fixed setting on an older tool and want a direct replacement, compare everyday grip, how you read the ready signal, and how you put the tool down between sections.

If the black UK Gold is on your shortlist alongside the Chronos, see ghd Gold vs Chronos for the differences in sleep timing, warranty and published plate widths.

Check the UK offer in front of you

Open the listing for the standard black model; then identify which model and size the seller has selected. Check whether the supplied plate guard or other accessories are stated on that offer. For a gift set, read the underlying styler name and plate width first, then decide whether you would use the extras. If you are considering a retailer other than ghd itself, compare the same model and condition and check the returns and warranty terms before comparing the prices directly.

If both choices cover your usual sections, the price difference matters more than a feature you would rarely use. If only one gives enough room to control the tool on the sections you care about, start with that model.
