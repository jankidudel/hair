Switch off the separate dryer and set it down safely. Let hot parts cool. For a manual round brush, stop pulling, support the hair near the barrel and gently reverse the last wrap a little at a time. If you cannot free it without force, pause and get in-person help. The last turn you made is the outermost one, so it is the first to undo.

If the brush itself is powered, switch it off and follow the release and safety instructions for your exact model. Stop if the hair will not release without force. A heated or rotating brush is a different appliance from a manual brush used with a separate dryer: Philips’s tangle support page for the BHH811/00 hot brush, for example, applies to that one model and describes no general release mechanism. Do not restart a jammed powered brush, open its casing or cut hair near the scalp. For a motor-wrapped chamber curler, use the automatic-curler method and its exact manual instead.

Find the last wrap, then reverse it

Release without adding tension

Keep the dryer away from the section. Look for the final direction of rotation and reverse just that movement. If a few loose hairs block the path, separate them gently with fingers before moving the brush again. Do not pull harder because the brush has stopped; that can tighten the wrap.

Prevent the same catch next time

Use a section narrower than the brush working area and keep neighbouring hair clipped away. Roll only as much as is needed to shape the section, then unroll along the same path rather than pulling the brush straight out of a coil. For long hair, begin with a smaller section and keep the ends aligned. The round-brush size guide helps you choose a barrel your section can wrap and leave without many extra turns.

Choose a manageable brush

A very small barrel can invite many turns on long hair; a very large one can feel hard to position on short layers. Match the brush to the hair length and desired bend, and check that the handle and barrel join feels smooth. A damaged brush that repeatedly traps hair should be replaced.

Work from the outside of the tangle

The last hair to wrap around the brush is usually the first to free. Find that outer strand and follow it toward the ends before moving the barrel. If hair from another section crossed over the brush, separate that loose hair first. Keep your fingers away from any recently heated dryer nozzle or brush surface until cool. A mirror or help from another person may make the wrapping direction easier to see.

Test the prevention step on one section

On your next blow-dry, choose a small section and count how many turns you need to make the bend you want. Note the direction you roll and deliberately unwind along the same path. Keep the neighbouring hair clipped away and stop before the ends pass over themselves repeatedly. If the brush still traps hair, examine its cool barrel and handle join for roughness and choose a brush with a smoother, more manageable path.
