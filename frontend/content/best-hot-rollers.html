Count the sections you want to set and the rollers in the sizes you will actually use. Compare the included clips and roller sizes before choosing a set for root lift, bends or a fuller set. A large total count does not help if most of the rollers are a size your plan does not need.

Quick answer

Choose the T3 Luxe for a short set of large rollers, such as root lift and soft bends on longer hair: it has eight rollers, all 1½ inches or larger. Choose the Conair HS400 for a fuller set with smaller curls: it has twenty rollers from ¾ to 1¼ inches and a clip for each.

Worked example: a plan that needs six of one size

Suppose your plan puts six 1½-inch rollers across the crown and top. Check each set against the size, not the total:

  1. T3 Luxe: four 1½-inch rollers. You are two short. You could fill the gap with two of the 1¾-inch rollers, but that changes the curve in those sections, so decide whether you would accept that.
  2. Conair HS400: no 1½-inch rollers at all. Its largest size is 1¼ inches. Twenty rollers do not cover a 1½-inch plan, so you would re-plan with eight 1¼-inch rollers instead.

Repeat the check for each size in your plan. A mixed set fails when the size you need most comes in the smallest count. Each roller also needs its own clip, and both sets include one clip per roller.

Partial set or full set

A partial set uses a few large rollers where you want lift or a soft bend, usually at the crown and around the face. Eight large rollers suit that job. A full set covers the whole head and usually mixes sizes, with smaller rollers at the nape and sides. Twenty rollers in three sizes suit that job, as long as the sizes match your plan.
