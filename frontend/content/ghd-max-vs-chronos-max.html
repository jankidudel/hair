Quick answer

The UK Max listing labels a 42 mm plate and the Chronos Max listing labels 43 mm. Both use a stated 185°C styling temperature. If your Max already gives you a section you can guide comfortably, the extra nominal millimetre alone supplies little reason to replace it. Consider Chronos Max when its documented control design addresses a specific priority, then check the selected kit and local electrical information.

Keep width attached to the measured surface

The width labels describe the maker's selected products. We have no same-method measurements of usable plate length, edge profile or the surface that actually contacts your section. A one-millimetre label difference cannot tell you how easily either tool reaches a short fringe. Before buying, compare how the closed body sits beside the roots and whether you can keep the section aligned from entry to release.

Compare the controls you will use

Max's dual-zone description and Chronos Max's motion-responsive description explain different control approaches. The listings do not offer a user-selected temperature range. If choosing your own setpoint is essential, neither fixed 185°C entry supplies that control. Product claims about speed or finish depend on the maker's stated test conditions. No CombReport timing, shine or retention measurements are supplied for this pair.

Make the upgrade calculation with your existing tool

Write down the unresolved task before comparing an offer. A working wide straightener may already cover a long section, while a smaller tool might provide access around the hairline. Changing to another wide body can repeat the same limitation. Compare the current complete package, protective storage and local instructions. An included guard and a separately advertised pouch are different purchase fields, so read the actual box list.
