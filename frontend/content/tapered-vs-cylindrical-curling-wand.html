A tapered wand changes diameter along its usable barrel; a cylindrical wand keeps a broadly constant diameter. If you want to vary where a section sits along one barrel, examine the taper. If you want one repeatable barrel curve across the working length, examine the cylinder. Both examples here are clipless wands, so the decision is profile rather than clamp versus wand.

The current US ghd Chronos Curve Conical Wand has a maker-labelled 1.1–0.9 inch tapered working barrel. The current US ghd Chronos Curve Max Wand is labelled a 1.5 inch barrel. The comparison is intentionally between these exact US product pages; it does not assume their headline dimensions are independently measured in the same way or promise that either makes a particular curl on your hair.

What placement changes

On a cylinder, moving the same section along the usable length does not deliberately change the barrel's nominal diameter. On a taper, placement closer to the wider base or narrower tip changes the curve presented to that section. This is a geometric option, not a controlled comparison of finished curl size. Hair tension, section thickness, wrapping path and release can change the result. Do not wrap onto the handle or assume that a cool tip is part of the styling length.

Try a section without overlapping it

Choose one representative section before changing tools. Check where it can sit along the usable barrel without reaching the handle or repeatedly covering itself. On the taper, note whether you can consistently return to the same area; on the cylinder, check whether the fixed working curve is already the one you want. Follow the exact glove, stand and release directions. If the section does not fit, reducing its width may solve the handling issue before a new barrel is needed.

Keep the model labels separate

The Chronos Curve Conical Wand's US page states a taper label and includes a glove, stand and 20-minute inactivity shutoff. The Chronos Curve Max Wand's US listing gives its 1.5-inch cylinder label, glove and 20-minute shutoff. Those controls may matter in a shortlist, but they do not isolate the effect of taper: the exact models also differ in nominal size. A fair profile choice starts with the intended section path, then checks each model's current controls and instructions. Neither page provides a universal heat setting to prescribe for a hair type.

The barrel-size owner explains diameter, length and unit labels in more detail. The clamp versus wand owner covers the separate question of how hair is held. If you already have a clipless wand and can place and release sections as intended, a taper is an option rather than a necessary upgrade. Try a representative section using the existing tool's exact directions before replacing it.

For either profile, follow the model's use, rest, cooling and storage instructions. The drawing shows geometry, not distance from skin or a contact method. The maker's curl duration and shine claims are not independent evidence that one profile performs better than the other.
