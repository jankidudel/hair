Choose a head that lets you guide your usual section from roots to ends and release it cleanly. A large oval can suit a broad bend, while a more manageable head may give you better access around layers. Check both on the shape and length you actually style. On long hair the end of each pass is where control matters: the section has further to travel, and the ends are what the brush must let go of.

Quick answer

Choose the head you can draw from roots to ends and release cleanly at the back of your head. Between the two US Revlon examples, the PLUS adds a detachable head and a named medium setting; the Original has a fixed head and three settings. Try the unplugged release check before buying either.

A holding and release check, tool off

With the brush unplugged, take one ordinary section from the back of your head.

  1. Place the section at the roots and draw the brush down to the ends.
  2. At the ends, turn the brush as you would to shape them, then let go of the section.
  3. Note whether the ends slide off the head or stay wrapped, and whether your wrist had to twist to reach the back.
  4. Repeat at a face-framing layer and at the crown.

If the head catches at release or you cannot reach the back comfortably, a smaller section or a different head geometry is the next thing to look at. The hot-air brush barrel size guide explains the measurements, and the long-hair hot-air brush tutorial covers the operating sequence.

Match the head to the shape you want

  • A broad bend through the lengths: a larger oval gives a wider curve as the section travels over it. Check that the ends still release at the bottom of the pass.
  • Shaped ends and face-framing layers: a head you can turn within a shorter section matters more than its size. Try the check above on your shortest layer, not only the longest length.
  • Root lift at the crown: you need to place the head close to the roots and lift. A detachable head does not change that geometry; it changes storage and how the head connects.

If you are choosing between the two Revlon models for long hair, the practical differences in the records are the detachable head and the extra named setting on the PLUS. Neither is a reason to buy on its own; they matter if you want to store the head separately or prefer a medium position between low and high.

When a separate dryer and brush is easier

A powered brush combines airflow and a brush head in one hand. On very long or layered hair, some people find it easier to rough-dry with a dryer first and then shape only the ends with a manual round brush, because each tool can be positioned separately. If your test above showed that the combined tool is awkward at the back of your head, that two-tool route may suit you better; see brushes for long hair for the manual option.
