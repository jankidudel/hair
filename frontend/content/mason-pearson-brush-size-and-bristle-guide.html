Choose the size and the bristle arrangement separately. Pocket, Handy, Junior and Popular describe formats, while codes such as B3, BN3 and SB3 identify different brush options. Two brushes can share a size and still have different tufts, so a size comparison alone is not enough to choose the model.

Quick answer

In the brushes checked here, the number names the size and the letters name the bristle option. The 3 brushes are Handy size and the 4 brushes are Pocket size. B is boar bristle, SB is the maker's fine boar-bristle “Sensitive” option, and BN is boar bristle mixed with nylon. In the bristle and nylon family, BN1 is the Popular, BN2 the Junior, BN3 the Handy and BN4 the Pocket. This pattern is confirmed only for the models in the table below; do not assume it for every product in the range.

Three worked choices

Same Handy size, different tufts: B3, SB3 or BN3

All three share the Handy dimensions and six rings of tufts, so size does not separate them. The choice is the bristle: stiff boar (B3), fine boar (SB3) or boar mixed with nylon (BN3). If you are deciding between natural bristle and a mix, read the boar-bristle versus nylon comparison first, then come back to choose the size.

Mixed bristle in Handy or Junior: BN3 or BN2

Both use boar bristle and nylon. The Junior (Medium) is 0.25 in wider than the Handy and its head is about 0.6 cm longer, with seven rings of tufts instead of six. Choose the Junior if you want a little more head per pass, and the Handy if you prefer the slightly smaller format. The difference in overall length is under 1 cm.

Pocket or a home-size brush

The Pocket is about 4.5 cm shorter overall than the Handy and has five rings of tufts. It suits a bag or a quick touch-up; a Handy or larger brush gives more head for brushing longer hair at home. Note also that the maker says the Handy comes with a cleaning brush and the Pocket does not, per the Pocket family page. For more on bag-sized formats, see travel hair brush formats.

Wet use and care boundaries

The bristle and nylon family page advises against using those brushes on wet hair and suggests a detangling comb instead. If you use one while heat styling, it says to turn the bristles away from the heat. That guidance is stated for the bristle and nylon family; check the page for a pure-bristle model rather than assuming the same rule. Natural boar bristle is an animal-derived material, and nylon is a different material choice, not a substitute with special properties.

For washing and drying a natural-bristle brush, use how to clean a boar-bristle hairbrush. For how boar bristle is built and used in general, see the boar-bristle brush guide.

When the smaller brush is enough

If you already own a larger brush you like, a Pocket adds portability rather than a different finish. If the Handy feels comfortable and covers your sections, moving up to a Junior or Popular changes head size, not the bristle type. Decide the bristle option first, then pick the smallest size that covers the way you brush. For a task-based starting point, see brushes for fine hair, or browse the brushes and combs guides.
