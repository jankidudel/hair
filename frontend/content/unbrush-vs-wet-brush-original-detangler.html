Quick answer

Compare these brushes by head shape, pin layout and the jobs each maker documents. Start with the brush you can hold comfortably while working through a small section, then check whether you need any separate dryer compatibility.

FHI Heat describes the UNbrush as having 105 DuoFlex bristles, 58 long and 47 mini, set in a backless vented cushion. Wet Brush describes the Original Detangler as using IntelliFlex bristles with an ergonomic design. Both makers say their brush works on wet or dry hair. The main difference you can read from the listings is the head: an open, vented cushion on the UNbrush and a single bristle type on the Wet Brush.

Choose by how you detangle

If you detangle small sections from the ends upward, you are mostly asking the brush to separate knots near the surface you can see. Both brushes are sold for that job. A reader who wants to rinse the brush easily after conditioner might favour the UNbrush’s backless, vented head, because water and product have somewhere to go. A reader who prefers a familiar paddle-style detangler with a single bristle type might start with the Wet Brush Original.

Hold each brush the way you would in the shower or at the mirror. Your thumb should rest without slipping while you draw the brush down a short section. The handle shape matters more than the bristle name if you cannot control the stroke.

Check the dryer question separately

Neither listing used here describes the brush as a blow-drying tool. If you want to use one brush while drying, compare the Original with a dryer-specific Wet Brush in Wet Brush Original Detangler versus Speed Dry, and read can you use a detangling brush for blow-drying before adding heat to any brush.

We have also left out the makers’ breakage percentages. FHI Heat and Wet Brush each cite a different test, so the numbers answer different questions and cannot rank one brush above the other.

Shopping check

Before buying, confirm three things on the retailer page: the exact name (UNbrush, not a different FHI Heat brush), the colour or set, and whether the item is the Original Detangler or a dryer-specific model. For a wider shortlist, see the best detangling brushes guide.

Worked example: a reader with long, thick hair who detangles in the shower and air-dries could compare the two heads in hand and pick the one that rinses clean more easily. A reader who wants one brush for detangling and rough drying should skip both originals and look at a brush whose maker describes dryer use.
