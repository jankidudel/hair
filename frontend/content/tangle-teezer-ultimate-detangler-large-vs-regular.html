Choose the head size you can control on one section. The Large version is aimed at longer, thicker or curlier hair. The regular size may suit a reader who prefers smaller sections or wants a more compact brush. Both are the same handled, two-tiered-tooth design for wet hair; size is the only choice on this page.

Quick answer

If you detangle long, thick or curly hair in broad sections, start with Large, which is what Tangle Teezer suggests. If you work in small sections, need to reach the nape easily or want a brush for a smaller bag, choose Regular. The brand publishes no measurements, so compare the two in person if the difference matters.

Check room at the nape and along a section

Two quick checks with dry, unbrushed hair tell you more than a size label:

  1. Nape room. Hold the brush (or your current brush of a similar size) behind your head and place it flat against the hairline at the nape. If the head catches on your shoulders or collar before the teeth sit flat, a smaller head will be easier to steer there.
  2. Section length. Take a section the width you normally use and lay the brush across it. If the section spills past both ends of the head, a larger brush covers it in fewer strokes. If the head is wider than the section, the extra width adds little.

If the two checks disagree, decide which area takes you longest. Many people find the back of the head is where a smaller brush helps most.

Regular Ultimate Detangler versus The Original

The names are easy to confuse. The Original is a separate, handle-free Tangle Teezer that sits in the palm and is described for wet and dry hair. The Ultimate Detangler has a handle and is described for wet hair and the shower. Choosing “regular” on the Ultimate Detangler page gives you the handled brush in its standard size, not The Original.
