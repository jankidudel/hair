<p>The right hair brush is the one that matches the job you want it to do. Buying a single expensive brush and expecting it to detangle, smooth and create a salon-style blow-dry is often where disappointment begins.</p>
<h2>Match the brush to the task</h2>
<h3>Detangling brush</h3>
<p>Choose this for everyday knots and gentle section-by-section brushing. Flexible bristles and a comfortable handle are useful starting points.</p>
<h3>Wide-tooth comb</h3>
<p>Choose this if you prefer open spacing and direct control, particularly in a damp routine. See <a href="/guides/wide-tooth-comb-vs-detangling-brush">Wide-Tooth Comb vs Detangling Brush</a>.</p>
<h3>Paddle brush</h3>
<p>Choose this for smoothing larger sections and a straighter direction during a blow-dry.</p>
<h3>Round brush</h3>
<p>Choose this for shaping, root lift and a more traditional blow-dry technique. Barrel size should match your hair length and desired finish.</p>
<h3>Boar-bristle or mixed-bristle brush</h3>
<p>Choose this mainly for smoothing and finishing, not as your only detangling tool. Read our <a href="/guides/boar-bristle-brush-guide">Boar-Bristle Brush Guide</a>.</p>
<h2>Consider your hair and routine</h2>
<p>Thick hair may need more spacing and a larger head; fine hair may benefit from a lighter brush and manageable barrel; curly hair routines often depend on when you choose to detangle. Start with the routine, not the trend.</p>
<h2>The bottom line</h2>
<p>You do not need a drawer full of brushes. Start with one good detangling tool, then add a styling brush only if it solves a real problem in your routine.</p>
