Quick answer

Consider Speed when its documented control lock, setting memory and magnetic Halo-nozzle setup answer a specific ownership question. Consider Helios when its variable controls and contoured-nozzle setup meet the task you need. Keep a working dryer when the proposed difference does not address a repeated problem.

Compare the documented interface

The UK Speed listing describes four heat options, four speed options, cool shot and memory associated with its control lock. The black Helios listing describes variable power and temperature controls with cool shot. Those descriptions establish interface features, not a head-to-head drying result.

Give setting memory a real job

If you repeatedly return to the same permitted control arrangement, decide whether recalling and locking it would reduce an awkward step. Check where the controls sit in the grip you use and how the lock is operated. This is a handling question you can rehearse with an unpowered display unit, not a finding about comfort from a product photograph.

If you rarely alter settings or your present controls are easy to reach, another interface may offer little practical value. More options do not prescribe a hotter setting or identify the right temperature for every section. Use the heat and speed guide for the separate control-use decision.

Include the nozzle in the purchase decision

Write down the attachment used for your main drying task. If you work with a separate brush, consider whether the selected nozzle can be positioned around the section without crowding the brush or your other hand. A different coupling and nozzle description can matter even when both products are sold as dryers.

Do not assume an attachment from Helios fits Speed because both carry the same brand. Verify the exact compatible part and the selected box. Additional accessories shown beside a product can be separate purchases. Diffuser compatibility owns the fit-versus-inclusion check when a diffuser is central to the routine.
