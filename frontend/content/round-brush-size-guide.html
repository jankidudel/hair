Choose a barrel your working section can follow and release from. Short face-framing sections often need a tighter curve than long lengths, but no length label supplies a personal diameter. The chooser above gives a small, middle or broader starting category only after you select length, desired shape and density. It does not measure your hair or certify a product size. High density changes how many sections you handle; it does not mechanically require a larger barrel.

Use the chooser as a section test

The small category is useful to test when a short section cannot follow a broad curve or you want a visible end bend. The middle category is a compromise when a bob or medium section reaches the cylinder and releases cleanly. A broader category can be tested for longer lengths and a looser shape, provided shorter layers do not skate off it. These are illustrative choices, not universal hair-type ranges. Root lift depends on placement, section direction and release as well as brush geometry.

Check exact labels before comparing products

A maker's size label can describe the solid barrel, the outside bristles or something else. Record what each label actually describes before comparing two brushes. Unknown definitions stay unknown: do not fill a gap with a number from another listing.

Worked comparison 1: two labels from different lines

CI-35 is labelled 1 3/8 inches and NT-34 is labelled 1 1/4 inches. The converted labels differ by about 3.2 mm, but because the two lines may not measure the same surface, that difference is not a proven gap in core size or in the curve your section will follow. Each page also presents a “Barrel & Bristle Diameters” image; check it before reading either label as a core diameter. The exact two-line comparison keeps those labels separate.

Worked comparison 2: the model number is not a measurement

The suffix 34 in NT-34 is not 34 mm: the label is 1 1/4 inches, which converts to 31.75 mm. The suffix 35 in CI-35 is close to its converted 34.9 mm label, but that is a coincidence of naming, not a rule. Use the stated label and its definition, never the model code.

Avoid comparing a powered hot-air attachment's outside head size with a manual brush core as if they were the same dimension. Powered oval and round heads have their own hot-air brush size guide.

Check grip and handle after choosing the curve

With an unpowered manual round brush, barrel size is only the first fit check. Hold the handle as you would while coordinating a separate dryer and rotate it slowly with the brush empty. Check whether your grip stays secure and whether the handle leaves room to turn without catching neighbouring hair. A larger decorative body or a thermal label does not establish comfortable control.

Then place one representative section against the brush. If the bristles do not catch it usefully, check section width and the actual bristle arrangement before buying a different diameter. For soft ends, the round-brush method explains the guided pass and release. The root-volume guide owns placement and lift. Choose a replacement only when the existing brush leaves a specific size, grip or release problem unresolved.

Test before adding another size

Try one visible front section with the brush you already own. If it catches the section without pulling neighbouring hair, guides it along the intended curve and releases cleanly, a different diameter may add little. If it does not, identify whether the problem is curve, working width, grip or sectioning before buying.

The named US maker listings supply nominal product labels. The chooser and illustrations express practical geometry, not hands-on measurement, predicted results or a performance ranking.
