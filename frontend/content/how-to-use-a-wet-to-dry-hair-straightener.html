Wet-to-dry straightening needs a sequence matched to the actual appliance. Identify the starting hair state, the mode that performs drying and any separate dry refresh before switching on. This guide organises those decisions; it does not turn every two-arm tool into the same styling method.

Quick answer

Use the exact model's preparation and electrical checks, organise the sections, and select its permitted drying mode. Follow the ready signal and documented movement. Complete the required drying stage before any dry-only finish, then shut down, unplug and cool as directed. A mode change is not permission to skip a preparation step.

Match the instructions before the session

Use the full model, generation and market on the appliance or its support record. A name reused across colours or revisions does not establish identical controls. Keep the actual manual available for safety checks, grip, resting position, settings and technique.

The current UK Airstrait Plum/Copper page shows an integrated comb and a separate Dry refresh. Do not fill in an unanswered control or handling detail from an older illustrated manual. Use the support route matched to your appliance.

Separate preparation from mode selection

The Duet Style guide is a named example requiring towel-dried hair rather than dripping hair for its drying operation. This is not a universal moisture percentage or a shared number of minutes for every wet-to-dry tool.

Apply only products permitted by their labels and the appliance instructions. Keep product distribution, detangling and the required hair state as separate checks. Heat-protectant application helps organise that handoff without inventing a dose or overriding either set of directions.

Make the working section easy to follow

Park unused lengths clear of the appliance's path, air openings and controls. Release the amount directed for the model rather than assuming a broad parked bundle should be styled at once. Keep finished areas recognisable so you do not lose track when putting the tool down.

Use the model's documented movement around roots and lengths; do not borrow a conventional iron's pass solely because the arms look similar. If the grip, section boundary or cord route becomes awkward, rest the appliance as directed and reorganise before continuing. Sectioning for styling owns that organisation task.

Keep an optional dry finish as a separate stage

For Duet Style, Shine Shot is dry-only. Keep it separate from the initial drying stage. It is optional when the intended result is already complete; a feature's presence is not a requirement to add another pass.

The Duet Style manual also has a ready sequence when returning from Shine Shot to drying. Follow that transition instead of assuming a button press instantly establishes the next operating condition. Other appliances need their own mode instructions; do not transfer the sequence by analogy.

Finish the session before maintenance or packing

Keep intake and outlet access clear as the manual directs. Stop for damage or abnormal operation rather than repeatedly changing modes to investigate. Use the manufacturer's support route for a fault; the table is not a repair procedure.

After the instructed shutdown and cooling, use wet-to-dry straightener care. For later travel, packing hair tools keeps electrical checks, cooled storage and arrival inspection together.
