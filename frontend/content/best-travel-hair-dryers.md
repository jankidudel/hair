<p>A travel hair dryer should earn its space in your bag. The best option is small enough to pack, simple enough to use abroad and capable enough that you do not regret leaving your regular dryer at home.</p>
<h2>Check voltage before anything else</h2>
<p>If you travel outside the UK, look for a dual-voltage model and read the manual before departure. A plug adapter changes the plug shape; it does not automatically change voltage. Never assume a dryer is safe to use overseas without checking its stated voltage range.</p>
<h2>What matters in a compact dryer</h2>
<p>Weight and folded size are obvious, but cable length and controls matter as well. A foldable handle can make packing easier. A concentrator nozzle is helpful if you want more directional airflow, while a mini diffuser can be worthwhile if your routine depends on one.</p>
<p>Expect a smaller dryer to be louder or less powerful than your usual full-size model. That is normal. The aim is practical drying on holiday, not recreating a salon-style routine in a hotel room.</p>
<h2>Buy for the trip you take</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Frequent international travel:</strong> dual voltage is essential.</li>
<li><strong>Weekend breaks:</strong> prioritise a small folded size and a simple nozzle.</li>
<li><strong>Longer trips:</strong> choose the model with the most useful controls, even if it is slightly larger.</li>
</ul>
<p>For a home tool, use our <a href="/guides/how-to-choose-hair-dryer">How to Choose a Hair Dryer</a> guide instead. If travel styling matters more than basic drying, compare a compact dryer with a <a href="/guides/best-hot-air-brushes">hot-air brush</a> before packing both.</p>
<h2>The bottom line</h2>
<p>Choose a travel dryer for compatibility and convenience first. A compact, dual-voltage model with sensible controls is usually more useful than a tiny dryer with no flexibility.</p>
