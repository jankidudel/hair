Choose your plate format before you sort through the black UK ghd range. The seven models covered here all advertise a styling temperature of 185°C. Their plate widths, inactivity shutoff times and warranty terms give you several ways to narrow the choice. Start with the section you find hardest to reach, then decide whether it calls for a narrow plate, a standard format or more working width. Keep the manufacturer’s individual product page and its interactive comparison separate in your notes. Those sources sometimes give different measurements for the same model, and the difference matters most when you need an exact fit.

Quick answer

Pick the plate format first. The ghd Mini (UK) is the narrow option; the Original, Gold, Platinum+ and Chronos (UK) are standard width; the Max and Chronos Max (UK) are wide. Within a format, compare the sleep timer and warranty: the two Chronos models sleep after 10 minutes, and Chronos and Platinum+ list three-year warranties. All seven advertise 185°C.

Compare within a format

The individual listings give every tool in the table a 2.7 m cord and an advertised styling temperature of 185°C. Within a format, compare the idle timer and warranty first if those are on your checklist. ghd’s comparison describes single-zone technology for Original (UK), dual-zone for Mini (UK), Gold (UK) and Max (UK), predictive Ultra-zone for Platinum+ (UK), and HD motion-responsive technology for Chronos (UK) and Chronos Max (UK). These are the maker’s control descriptions. They give you a way to identify the models within each width group.

If heat-up time is part of your checklist, ghd’s interactive comparison gives 20 seconds for Platinum+ (UK) and Chronos (UK), 25 seconds for Mini (UK) and Gold (UK), 30 seconds for Original (UK) and Max (UK), and 35 seconds for Chronos Max (UK). Keep those switch-on figures in a separate column from the idle timer. Decide which setting or term would actually affect your daily routine before you compare every number on the list.

Three ways to choose

If fringe access decides the purchase: Put the ghd Mini (UK) on the shortlist as the narrow option. Look at the room around the section you want to reach. If you work with a fringe and longer sections on the same day, include both when you check the tool’s placement. A fit check can help if any of the three published widths would change your decision.

If you want a familiar standard format: Compare the stated terms for the ghd Original (UK), Gold (UK), Platinum+ (UK) and Chronos (UK). The ghd Chronos (UK) has the shorter idle interval of this group. Two pairs are compared in detail: ghd Gold vs Chronos and ghd Mini vs Original. If you want a wider plate: Start with the ghd Max (UK) and ghd Chronos Max (UK). They share a two-year warranty and have different idle intervals. If you are deciding whether to go wide at all, see ghd Chronos vs Chronos Max.
