A flat iron or straightener may catch because a section is tangled or too wide, the plates are pressed unevenly, residue has collected, or an edge is damaged. Note exactly where the hair catches. If you can see chipped coating, a gap, a loose part, or damaged cord, stop using the tool and contact its maker; do not try to reshape or repair it.

If the plates are intact and the manual permits external cleaning, follow How to clean hair-straightener plates before reassessing the snag.

First checks

  1. Turn off, unplug and let the tool cool before inspecting it.
  2. Detangle a narrow section without forcing the tool through knots.
  3. Look at the plate edges and hinge in good light; do not touch internal parts.

Match the sign to an adjustment

Catch location Likely issue to inspect Action
At entry to the plates Section too wide or tangled Reduce section width and detangle first
Repeatedly at one plate edge Visible residue, roughness, gap or chipped surface Clean only as the manual permits; stop if damaged
Near hinge or outer housing Hair caught outside intended plate area Keep section centered; discontinue use if a part is loose

Separate technique from tool condition

If snagging disappears with a smaller detangled section and light, even closure, technique may be involved. If it recurs at the same physical point, inspect the cool plates and edges. Do not continue to pull hair through a visibly damaged surface.

Clean only by the manual

After unplugging and cooling, compare the exact model’s cleaning instructions. A ghd Original manual allows a damp cloth on the exterior and prohibits immersion and abrasive cleaning. That is an example, not permission to use water or solvents on every iron. Let any permitted surface dry fully before use.

When to seek support

Sanding plates, bending a gap closed, dismantling the housing, or pressing harder can make the problem worse. Ask the manufacturer about a damaged plate, loose hardware, unusual smell or electrical fault.

Use the location to narrow the next check

A catch near the roots on the first pass may come from a knot or a section wider than the usable plate area. A catch at the same edge even on a narrow detangled section points toward the physical tool. A catch near the hinge can happen when hair is fed outside the plates. Note the location with the tool off, then look again only after it is fully cool. You do not need to keep pulling hair through to confirm a repeatable snag.

Avoid improvised surface fixes

Scraping residue with metal, sanding a rough plate, or bending the housing may damage the finish or create a new gap. Even when residue seems likely, follow the exact maker cleaning directions and let the tool dry fully if its manual permits a damp wipe. If the surface remains rough, the plate coating is chipped, or the tool no longer closes evenly, ask the maker about service or replacement rather than using extra pressure.
