Check what was weighed before comparing numbers. A dryer body figure can exclude its nozzle, cord and plug. Also consider handle reach, cable pull and whether you can operate the controls while holding the dryer where you use it. The three UK listings below show the problem plainly: one states its weighing basis, one gives a number with no basis, and one gives no weight in the record CombReport holds.

Quick answer

Compare weights only when the listing says what was weighed, then check handling yourself with the attachment fitted. In the UK listings checked, GA.MA gives 294 g without the cable, Shark gives 0.73 kg with no basis, and no Dyson weight is recorded, so none of them is ranked here.

What a weight figure can leave out

  • Cable and plug: a long cord adds weight and also pulls on the dryer as it hangs. GA.MA’s 294 g explicitly excludes the cable.
  • The attachment you always use: a nozzle or diffuser sits at the far end of the dryer, so it changes the balance as well as the total.
  • A packaged weight: some listings give a shipping or boxed weight. That is not what you hold.

When a listing only gives a bare number, ask the maker what it includes, or compare handling directly instead.

An unplugged handling check

With the dryer switched off and unplugged, fit the attachment you would use most, then hold the dryer where you normally dry the back of your head.

  1. Hold that position for as long as one section usually takes.
  2. Reach the heat, speed and cool controls without changing your grip.
  3. Let the cord hang as it would from the socket and notice whether it pulls the dryer’s angle.
  4. Repeat at the crown and the nape.

If one position is awkward, a lighter number may not fix it; a different handle angle, a shorter attachment or a better cord route might. This is your own handling observation, not a weighing or a CombReport test.

When the number matters, and when it does not

Weight matters most when you hold the dryer up for a long time: long or dense hair, a slow section-by-section routine, or drying the back of the head with your arm raised. For a quick rough-dry of short hair, balance and control placement often matter more than a small difference in grams.

If you already own a dryer that feels heavy, try the handling check with a shorter attachment or no attachment where the manual allows, and change how the cord hangs. If that fixes the strain, the dryer was not the problem.
