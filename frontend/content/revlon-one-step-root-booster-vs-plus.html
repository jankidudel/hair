Quick answer

Start with the round Root Booster for a fringe, shorter layers or a small area near the roots. Start with the oval Volumizer Plus if your main job is smoothing and shaping longer sections. The purchase comes down to how much room you have to turn the brush on the shortest section you want to style.

Check a fringe, a bob and long lengths

For a fringe, picture the brush held across the smallest workable section. If the head crowds the angle you need, start with the smaller round shape. For a bob, look for room to turn the brush at the ends without taking a second wrap. For long layers, compare how many sections you would divide the hair into with each head. A compact head can suit the front while a broad head suits the bulk of the routine.

Use the starting-state instructions supplied with the exact brush. If the store calls the smaller tool Style Booster, check its local code and plug before relying on the US RVDR5292 listing.

Check the shape the barrel measurement leaves out

Use a familiar brush as a reference. Hold it beside the front section you normally style and notice how much of the round or oval surface the hair can reach. Then look at the thickness of the bristles around the head and where the handle begins. The listing’s headline barrel dimension can help you compare, but the clearance you need is the space taken up by the full head at the angle you actually use.

For example, suppose your current large oval brush works through the back of your hair, yet you tend to push your fringe to one side because the brush will not turn comfortably underneath it. The Root Booster is worth considering for that one part of the routine. If longer sections keep falling off a small round brush before you finish the pass, the Plus may be a better candidate for the lengths. Choose which of those jobs would justify buying the tool and judge the control layout on that job first.

Use the model code when shopping

Open the product details and check the model code, head shape and market. Then look at the photos to find the selector in relation to the handle. If storage is important, check whether the page explicitly documents removal and give the tool room to cool first. These few checks can help you spot a retailer listing that uses a One-Step family name beside a photo or description for a different brush.
