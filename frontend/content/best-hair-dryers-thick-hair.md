<p>Thick hair can make a weak dryer feel like a bad investment very quickly. The aim is not simply maximum heat. It is enough airflow to work through sections efficiently, paired with controls that let you adapt the dryer to your routine.</p>
<h2>The features worth paying for</h2>
<p>Look for a full-size dryer with multiple airflow and heat settings. A stronger motor or high-airflow design can reduce drying time, but comfort matters too: a heavy dryer becomes tiresome when you are working through a full head of hair.</p>
<p>A concentrator nozzle is the sensible default for smoothing and directional blow-drying. If you wear curls or waves, consider a diffuser that fits securely and distributes air broadly. Check replacement attachment availability as well; it is an unglamorous detail that matters over time.</p>
<h2>A more efficient routine</h2>
<p>Divide hair into manageable sections before switching the dryer on. Start at the roots, where trapped moisture slows the whole job down. Use high airflow and moderate heat first, then lower the heat for the final smoothing pass. This is usually more pleasant than holding maximum heat over the same area.</p>
<p>Use a heat-protection product as directed and keep airflow moving. A good dryer should make thick hair easier to manage, not encourage rushing through large, damp sections.</p>
<h2>Choose by your outcome</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Smooth blow-dry:</strong> full-size dryer, concentrator nozzle, multiple controls.</li>
<li><strong>Defined curls or waves:</strong> secure diffuser, broad airflow, adjustable heat.</li>
<li><strong>Long hair with frequent wash days:</strong> prioritise weight, cable length and drying efficiency.</li>
</ul>
