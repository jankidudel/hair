Choose Airsmooth if you want to guide a section through a bristled styling brush. Choose Airstrait if you want to guide it through straightening arms. Both have documented uses on wet and dry hair. Check the mode for the way you plan to start, and choose the hand movement you would actually use. This page compares the standalone UK Airsmooth in Ceramic pink/Rose gold with the UK Airstrait in Plum/Copper.

Quick answer

Pick Airsmooth for a brush-led finish with bend or lift at the ends, and Airstrait for a straight finish drawn between arms. Both listings document a wet-to-dry use and a separate dry-hair option, so the deciding question is the movement and finish, not whether you start wet. Keep the tools you have if they already give that finish.

Three Dyson names that are easy to mix up

  • Airsmooth: a standalone styling brush with a fixed, non-detachable teardrop head. This is the brush on this page.
  • AirSmooth2x: a similarly named attachment supplied in some Airwrap Co-anda2x kits. It is not the standalone appliance and is not compared here.
  • Airstrait: a straightener with two arms that uses airflow rather than hot plates.

For the Co-anda2x attachment question, start from the Airwrap attachment compatibility lookup instead.

Choose by the movement you will repeat

Picture one ordinary section. With a brush, you place the section over the bristles and draw it along while the head turns or lifts it; the shape at the ends depends on how you roll or release. With straightening arms, you close the section between them and draw down; the movement is closer to a flat iron and gives a straighter line rather than a brush bend.

  • Brush-led finish: you want some bend or lift at the ends and like a blowout movement. Look at Airsmooth.
  • Straight finish with arms: you already straighten after drying and want to combine the two stages. Look at Airstrait.
  • A tool you own already does the job: keep it. A round-brush routine with a dryer, or a flat iron on dry hair, is not replaced by a newer category unless the combined stage is what you want.

Rehearse the movement unpowered with a display model where you can. Hold a manageable section and notice whether you prefer rolling it over a head or pressing it between arms. That is a handling preference, not a test result.

Check the mode for how you start

Both listings separate a wet-to-dry use from a dry-hair option. On the Airsmooth that is a dry-refresh direction; on the Airstrait it is a named Dry mode. Decide which you would use most: if you mainly style straight from washing, the wet-to-dry path is the one to read in the manual; if you mainly refresh second-day hair, read the dry option. Follow the supplied instructions for the mode rather than assuming one setting covers both.
