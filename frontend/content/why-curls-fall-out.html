First identify when the styled shape changes: as it leaves the tool, before you separate it, during brushing or later in the day. Those are different observations. A deliberately softened wave can be the intended finish, while a curl that never forms needs a different check from one that changes during finishing.

Quick answer

Check the tool's permitted starting state, the shape you intended, the section you worked and whether the curl cooled before separation. Compare one stage at a time. Do not automatically raise heat, repeat passes or add more product when the result changes.

Locate the change before changing the routine

Watch one manageable section rather than judging only the final head of hair. Note its shape immediately after release, once cooled and after your chosen separation step. You are making a practical observation, not a controlled retention test.

These are observation prompts, not established causes. The same visible result can follow different routines, so do not assign a single cause from appearance alone.

Check the original shape and section

A broad starting bend and a compact curl should not be judged against the same expectation. Use barrel size for that shape decision. A different tool is worth considering only when it offers the geometry or handling you actually need.

Follow the exact appliance's sectioning, wrapping and release instructions. If only the outside of a bundle follows the tool, review how the section is arranged rather than compensate with longer contact or an automatic setting increase. Clamp iron versus wand owns the mechanism comparison.

If the original shape came from a pressed pattern, compare waver and curling-iron mechanisms before applying a wrapped-barrel explanation. Waver use owns its next-placement sequence. A deliberately softer pattern after finishing is a different observation from hair that was never placed as intended.

Keep cooling separate from brush-out

The US Conair CD705/CD706 wand manual specifies dry hair and tells users not to comb or brush the formed curl until cooled. That is a named wand example, not a universal recipe for every curling appliance.

The ghd Australian curl tutorial also separates cooling from brushing into waves. If the change happens when you brush, use how to brush out curls to try the intended separation on one cooled section first. Brushing is allowed to change the shape; decide how much change you wanted.

Review the product routine without guessing a cause

Record the exact products, their permitted application state and the amounts used. Keep a product only when it has a clear compatible role. The tool's preparation restrictions still apply to anything left from an earlier stage; a dry-looking finish does not establish permission.

Use styling product order for that planning step. If you are considering another finishing spray, compare flexible and strong hold labels instead of taking more hold as proof that the original problem is solved.

Make the next attempt interpretable

Keep the tool and most of the routine familiar, then change one useful handling choice within the instructions. Note whether you changed the section, release or amount of separation. If you replace the barrel, change products and brush differently together, the result cannot identify which choice mattered.
