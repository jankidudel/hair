Quick answer

The B1 uses a 1-inch barrel and the B1.25 uses a 1.25-inch barrel. Compare how your shortest layer can wrap around the two sizes and how the clamp and rotation controls fit the way you curl.

Beachwaver lists the same supporting details for both: a ceramic barrel 5.5 inches long, three heat settings of 290°F, 350°F and 410°F, left and right arrow buttons that rotate the barrel only while you hold them, a beveled clamp, an 8 ft swivel cord and a 30-minute automatic shut-off. The maker describes the B1 as its most popular size and the B1.25 as giving a looser, blow-out wave for longer hair.

What a rotating clamp barrel does

A Beachwaver is a clamp curling iron whose barrel turns for you. You open the clamp, secure it near the ends of a section, then hold an arrow button so the barrel winds the hair. Let go and it stops. After a two to three second hold, open the clamp and slide the barrel straight down. You do not turn your wrist, and the arrows let you mirror the curl direction on each side of your face.

That is a different format from an automatic curler that draws hair into a chamber. If you are weighing those formats rather than two barrel sizes, read automatic hair curler versus curling wand first.

Choose the size with your shortest layer

A larger barrel needs more length to make one full wrap. Beachwaver says short hair is harder: at chin length or above you get less curl and less room to move, so it suggests smaller sections, clamping close to the ends and expecting bend rather than a full ringlet. On those short layers, the 1-inch B1 leaves more hair free to wrap than the 1.25-inch B1.25.

Short-layer example: your front layers end at the chin and the rest of your hair sits on the collarbone. The B1 is the more practical size for the shorter pieces, and Beachwaver names collarbone length as a good starting point for it.

Long-section example: your hair falls well below the shoulders and you want a loose, brushed-out bend. Beachwaver says the B1.25 covers more hair per section and gives a looser wave, so it suits that aim. Take wide sections, curl from the mid-lengths down and brush through once cool.

Start from the maker’s directions

Beachwaver says hair must be fully dry, and suggests sections about an inch to an inch and a half wide and no thicker than the barrel. Start on the lowest setting and go up only if the curl will not hold. Let each curl cool before touching it. Clean the barrel with a damp cloth only once it is completely cool, and do not wind the cord around a hot barrel.
