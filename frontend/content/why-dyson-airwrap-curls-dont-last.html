Quick answer

Identify the first point where the result goes wrong. Hair that never wraps around the barrel needs a different check from a curl that forms and relaxes later. Follow the styling sequence for your Airwrap and barrel, then change one part of the routine on the next section.

Find the first change

Table: swipe sideways on a small screen, or focus and use arrow keys.

What happens What to check next
The ends fail to wrap Try a smaller working section. Confirm the barrel direction and the starting hair state in Dyson’s guide for your model.
The curl drops as you release it Check that you completed the curling and cooling sequence and released the section as the instructions show.
The shape changes during brush-out Compare how the curl looks at release with how it looks before brushing. Try letting the next section settle before handling it.
The curl opens up later Check whether it had the expected shape before you left it alone. Use the general curl retention guide to review what changed after styling.

Test one ordinary section at a time

Start with two similar sections and use the same barrel, preparation and controls. Leave the first section as you usually style it. On the second, change only the working section size or one step in the documented sequence. Compare both sections immediately after release and again after you have finished the rest of your hair.

For Airwrap i.d., check the MyDyson setup if you rely on the connected curling sequence. For manual use, check the instructions that match the body and barrel in your hand. Use the attachment compatibility guide if the barrel does not fit, or the Airwrap i.d. vs Co-anda2x comparison if you are choosing an upgrade.

Choose one change for the stage that failed

If the ends never pull around the barrel, start with pickup. Check the starting hair state in the Dyson directions and repeat on a smaller section with the same barrel. Note whether it takes hold and stays in position as you complete the sequence. If you change the barrel, the section and the styling settings together, you will have a harder time working out which difference helped.

If a shape forms but drops immediately, record whether you completed the model’s cooling and release sequence. Look at the curl as it leaves the tool and again once the section has settled. If the second look is where you first see a difference, work through what happened in between. For example, you might have held it while moving to the other side of your head or brushed it out before finishing the remaining sections. Compare sections with the same approach at that stage before moving on to another barrel.

Record the connected and manual routines separately

If you use Airwrap i.d., write down whether you started the connected curl sequence or used the controls manually. A comparison between the two can help you determine which step is easier to repeat in your routine. Use the same preparation and a similar section on both sides, and note the result at release, after handling and later in the day. On another Airwrap generation, check the directions for that tool before trying a sequence you saw described for i.d.

If the curl still relaxes later while both test sections initially look the way you want, use the retention guide above. It helps you review what happened after the curl was made. Keep the barrel and starting state in your notes so you can repeat the result you had at release.
