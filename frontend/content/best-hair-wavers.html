Quick answer

Choose the head you can position around your shortest working section. The Mermade 25 mm Mini Pro Waver gives you a smaller option within that range, while its 32 mm Original Pro Waver gives you a larger barrel. The amika High Tide is another deep-waver shape worth comparing if the whole head fits comfortably around the section.

Try a placement check first

With the appliance cool and unplugged, hold it beside the shortest section you want to wave. Check whether you can place the whole section into the head and still leave room away from the scalp and ears. Then picture where the next clamp would go. If the first placement uses most of the section, a smaller head or a different format may give you more control.

For the heated pass, follow your model’s directions and use the starting hair state it specifies. Work through how to use a hair waver if you need help placing the next wave. See hair waver vs curling iron if you want to choose between a repeated wave and individual curls.

Choose for the first placement and the repeat pattern

Suppose your hair is around shoulder length and the front pieces are a little shorter. Check first whether the head will fit midway down one of those front pieces. Then check whether you would realistically have room for another placement below it. If the front has room for one placement and the back has room for several, decide whether you want waves throughout or mainly through the longer sections. That decision can tell you whether a narrower waver or a different styling tool belongs on your shortlist.

For a deeper bend over longer hair, look at how the barrels sit across the section, where the outer edge of the clamp falls and which direction you would move after release. The size in a product name helps when comparing variants from the same range. Across ranges, photos and instructions can give a clearer sense of how much of the section sits inside the tool.

Compare controls while the tool is out of use

With a cool tool unplugged, notice where your thumb lands when you open the clamp. Look for a control layout that lets you keep your fingers on the handle and use it as the maker describes. On the exact UK listing, check whatever settings and indicators the maker documents. A comfortable grip is worth checking separately from the advertised wave size, especially if you plan to repeat the movement across your whole head.
