Cleaning a hot-air brush is mostly about removing trapped hair and keeping the brush head and air-intake areas clear. Use it as an electrical tool, not as a washable hair brush: unplug it, let it cool completely, and do not run water into the body, vents or electrical connection.

Regular cleaning makes the tool easier to use. Hair wrapped around the bristles can snag sections and reduce control, while dust or lint around air vents can interfere with airflow. A short clean after a few uses and a more careful check when buildup is visible is enough for most people.

What you need

Have a dry microfiber cloth or soft cloth, a comb tail or blunt pointed tool for lifting trapped hair, and a small dry soft brush available. Check the manufacturer instructions before removing a detachable head or using any cleaner. If the instructions say a part is washable, follow that guidance; otherwise keep moisture away from the appliance.

Step 1: unplug and cool it completely

Switch the tool off, unplug it and place it somewhere stable until it is cool. Do not clean it while warm or while it is connected to power. This also makes trapped hair easier to handle without pulling it against hot bristles.

Step 2: remove trapped hair gently

Start at the outer edge of the brush head. Lift a small amount of hair with the comb tail or your fingers and unwind it rather than tearing it through the bristles. Work around the barrel in short sections. If a large amount has built up, remove it in layers so you do not bend flexible bristles or pull at a detachable attachment.

Avoid sharp blades. They can cut bristles, scratch coatings or damage the mesh around an intake. A slow, gentle approach is safer and normally quick once you do it regularly.

Step 3: wipe the barrel and bristles

Use a dry cloth first to remove loose dust. For a small amount of residue, use a cloth that is only barely damp, not wet, and wipe the external plastic or barrel surface carefully. Do not spray cleaner directly on the brush, and do not let liquid run into gaps, the handle or air openings. Wipe again with a dry cloth and leave the tool fully dry before its next use.

If the brush head is detachable, remove it only as the instructions describe. A removable attachment may have its own cleaning advice; do not assume every attachment can be rinsed.

Step 4: check the intake and vents

Look for lint or dust around the air-intake cover and any visible vent openings. Use a dry soft brush to loosen surface debris. Do not poke deeply into the tool and do not use water, compressed liquid cleaners or metal objects. If the intake cover is designed to come off, follow the manufacturer method and put it back securely before using the appliance.

How often should you clean it?

Remove visible hair whenever it begins to collect. A quick check every few uses prevents a tedious deep clean later. Check the intake more often if you use styling products that leave residue, have pets or store the tool in a dusty drawer.

Who this is for / choose something else if…

This is routine external care for a working hot-air brush. Stop and use the manufacturer support guidance if the tool smells unusual, has damaged parts, gets excessively hot, has a loose cable or no longer produces normal airflow. Cleaning is not a repair method.

Clean only the parts your model permits

A detachable brush head is not automatically washable. Check whether the manual permits removing hair from the bristles, cleaning an accessible vent, or wiping a particular surface, and keep the electrical body dry unless the maker explicitly says otherwise. If residue or trapped hair remains after permitted care, ask the maker before using a sharp tool or liquid. For a focused next step, see the frizz troubleshooting after cleaning.
