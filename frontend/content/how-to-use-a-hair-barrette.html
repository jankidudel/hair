A conventional clasp barrette closes a hinged fastening beneath a decorative front. Learn that fastening's release before putting it into your hair, then practise on one arrangement you can support. A bend-open snap clip has a different action and needs a different method.

Quick answer

Open the clasp using its normal release, support the intended section, guide the lower bar underneath it and close the latch without trapping loose strands. Check the hold before letting go. To remove it, support the hair again and open the fastening before withdrawing the barrette.

Identify the fastening in your hand

Look underneath the decorative front. Find the hinge, lower bar and latch, then open and close the empty piece so you know where to press. Set aside an accessory with a rough edge, distorted bar or damaged catch. Do not bend the fastening to create extra room.

The TEGEN own-range tutorial describes a conventional clasp released with side pads. That example establishes one mechanism; another barrette may release differently. If the mechanism is unclear, use its maker's instructions before placing it.

Begin with one supported arrangement

A gathered half-up section or another section suited to the actual clasp can be a manageable first attempt. Keep the arrangement supported with one hand while you position the accessory. A small side piece and a full low ponytail present different amounts at the fastening, even on the same head.

Choose the gathered amount for the intended style rather than the decorative front's length. Barrette selection owns clearance and fit. A long front does not prove that the clasp can close around a large bundle.

Place, close and check

  1. Form the chosen section and keep the strands you do not want to capture clear of the hinge and latch.
  2. Open the barrette normally, keeping the release accessible.
  3. Guide the lower bar beneath the supported section without scraping the scalp or collecting neighbouring hair.
  4. Bring the top and lower bar together through the intended closing action. If the latch resists, reopen it instead of forcing the decorative front down.
  5. Ease away the supporting hand and check the arrangement with ordinary gentle movement.
  6. If it shifts or presses, support the section again, release the clasp and change the placement before another attempt.

TEGEN documents passing the lower part beneath a supported ponytail and closing the clasp above it. This guide uses that basic action without promising that every barrette can carry a full ponytail or twist.

Separate two opposite fit problems

What you observe First check What to avoid
Latch cannot close normally Gathered amount, placement and mechanism condition Squeezing harder or bending the bar
Clasp closes but slides Useful contact with the actual section Automatically reducing the amount further
Hair catches at the hinge Loose strands outside the intended bundle Pulling the closed accessory through them
One point presses Position and direction of contact Taking discomfort as proof of secure hold

If the section is too broad for closure, reconsider the arrangement or choose suitable clearance. If a roomy clasp makes little contact with a small section, a different placement or closer-fitting fastening may be the useful comparison. These observations do not establish a universal hair-type rule.

Release before taking the style down

Support the arrangement, operate the release and open the lower bar clear of the captured hair. Guide the barrette away without dragging the latch through the length. If a strand catches, pause and free it before continuing.

For a different finished-style mechanism, use snap-clip placement or claw-clip use. For care, follow clip and pin cleaning; a metal-looking clasp does not establish that the complete decorated piece can be soaked.
