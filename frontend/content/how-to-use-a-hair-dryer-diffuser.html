A diffuser spreads dryer airflow across a section instead of concentrating it in one direction. Start with hair that is comfortably blotted, choose moderate settings and work in sections you can hold without rushing.

Set up the dryer

Fit the diffuser securely, then begin with moderate heat and airflow. Settings are personal: the useful choice is one that feels controllable while you work around the roots and ends.

Before switching on, check that the attachment sits firmly on the dryer and that the cable gives you room to move without pulling. Begin with freshly blotted hair rather than hair that is dripping wet, then choose a setting you can keep in one place for a few seconds. Starting gently makes it easier to learn how your dryer and attachment feel together.

Hold the dryer by its handle and let the bowl support the section rather than pressing it against your head. Keep the attachment clear of loose clothing, towels and the cord. If the airflow feels too forceful to position calmly, lower it before moving on; control is more useful than trying to finish every section as quickly as possible.

Dry in manageable sections

Bring a section gently toward the bowl of the diffuser and pause rather than constantly moving the attachment through it. Move to the next section once it feels mostly dry, then revisit areas that need more time.

Start underneath and work upward so you can keep finished sections separate from the hair you are still drying. Gather only enough hair to rest easily in the bowl, then pause with the dryer angled toward the section. This gives the broadly spread air time to reach it without repeatedly pulling the hair in different directions.

Change the placement when you want to reach a new area rather than sweeping through the same section without a plan. Around the roots, tilt the bowl so it is close enough to direct air there while remaining comfortable to hold. Alternate sides if that makes the routine easier, and take a short pause when you need to reset the next section.

Adjust for your preferred finish

Use a little less airflow when you want a calmer finish, or change the angle when you need to reach the roots. The aim is an ordinary styling routine, not a fixed technique to follow perfectly.

For more lift, bring a section toward the diffuser at the roots before letting it fall into place. For a looser, more relaxed result, keep the dryer slightly farther away and avoid disturbing the section once it has started to settle. The same attachment can support several looks, so use a small change in angle or section size before assuming you need a different tool.

Finish by checking whether any areas still feel damp and giving only those a short final pass. Once you are happy with the overall shape, allow the hair to cool and settle before brushing or rearranging it. This keeps the routine focused on the finish you prefer instead of adding repeated passes by habit.

Make the routine repeatable

The most useful diffuser routine is one you can repeat without overthinking it: secure the attachment, use manageable sections, start at a controlled setting and adjust the angle for the shape you want. Notice which section size and airflow feel easiest, then use those as your starting point next time.

A diffuser is one attachment choice, not a rule for every dry. If you prefer a more directed finish on another day, a concentrator may make more sense. Matching the attachment to the style you want keeps the dryer useful across different ordinary routines.

What you notice Try next Reason
The bowl feels crowded Use a smaller section The air can move around it more evenly
Airflow feels too lively Lower it before changing technique It is easier to hold the section in place
Roots need more attention Change the bowl angle and pause there briefly You can reach the area without sweeping through it

See these as small adjustments, not rules to get right on the first try. A repeatable routine comes from finding a section size and setting that feel easy to handle, then making one change at a time when you want a different look. Use a cool shot at the end of a section if you want the shape to settle before moving on.

Who this guide is for

This guide is for someone who has a compatible diffuser and wants a calm, practical starting routine. It is not a requirement that every curl or wave be dried this way.
