A conventional dry-use flat iron works on a prepared section between heated plates. Organise the hair, workspace and route before the pass, then review alignment rather than pressing harder or repeating a catch. A steam system or wet-to-dry appliance needs its own instructions and method.

Quick answer

Follow the exact iron's preparation and settings, separate a manageable dry section, close the plates through their normal movement and guide a continuous pass toward the ends. Release clear of the tips. Stop for a catch and inspect the cause before continuing; there is no universal pass count or speed.

Confirm the task and starting state

Use this method for a conventional dry-use plate tool. The original Chronos safety guide supplies a named completely-dry-hair requirement. Your own appliance's instructions control its preparation, permitted products, ready state and grip.

If the intended tool has a reservoir, use steam-straightener operation. For a combined drying system, use wet-to-dry straightener operation. Neither mechanism inherits this page's sequence from a similar outline.

Prepare the section and the place to rest the tool

Resolve tangles with a suitable ordinary brush or comb. Apply only compatible products as their labels direct and meet the iron's preparation requirements afterwards. Heat-protectant application explains why distribution and readiness are separate checks.

Park unused hair clear of the working path. Present a section you can keep flat within the usable plate area, rather than a round bundle spilling past its edges. Leave the cord a clear route and place the tool's instructed resting surface where you can use it without losing the section boundary.

Select any available setting through the maker's guidance. A more crowded section is not automatically a reason to raise heat. The control guide separates user selection from internal regulation without assigning a shared temperature chart.

Guide the pass and release deliberately

Position the section as the model directs, keeping loose neighbouring strands clear of the hinge and body. Close normally without extra force, maintain alignment and move toward the ends without stopping to clamp one place more firmly. Release clear of the tips rather than pulling against a closed tool.

The US ghd Max straightening tutorial is a named sectioning and moving-pass example. Its particular comb-led technique, product and section measurement are not requirements for every iron. Follow your own tool rather than copying an unsupported distance from the scalp or a tutorial's exact pace.

For a turn-and-travel curl instead of straightening, use the separate flat-iron curl method. A different styling movement should be chosen deliberately, not introduced to compensate for a snag.

Review one section before repeating the plan

Choose an area where you can see the tool enter and leave. Notice whether the section remained flat, the movement stayed continuous and hair escaped at an edge. If alignment fails, let the section settle and change its breadth or clipping plan before another attempt. Repeated passes are not a target.

Keep finished hair separate from unworked sections. For a shorter piece, review the amount presented and the housing's access rather than forcing the appliance closer than its directions permit. Plate width concerns usable contact, not simply the outside body measurement.

Stop for a mechanical problem and finish the session

A repeated catch at the same tool location calls for shutdown and cold inspection. Snagging troubleshooting separates section handling from a plate edge, hinge or body problem. Do not file a surface, force a hinge or add an unapproved coating.

Use plate care only through the model's permitted cleaning method. A damaged heated surface, housing, cord or plug needs the stop-use and service route rather than stronger cleaning. Complete the instructed shutdown, unplugging, cooling and storage after a normal session too.
