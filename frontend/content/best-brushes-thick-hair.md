<p>Thick hair can overwhelm a brush that is too small, too dense or too rigid. The useful question is what job the brush needs to do: detangle, smooth, distribute product or support a blow-dry.</p>
<h2>For detangling</h2>
<p>Choose a brush with enough space and flexibility to work through sections without feeling stuck. Start at the ends and work upward. A wide-tooth comb may also suit a wet-detangling routine, depending on your preference.</p>
<h2>For smoothing</h2>
<p>A paddle brush can be useful for larger sections and a straighter finish. Look for a comfortable handle and a head size you can control; huge brushes are not automatically easier on dense hair.</p>
<h2>For blow-drying</h2>
<p>Round brushes give more shape but require more technique. Choose a barrel size based on your length and the amount of bend you want. Pair it with a concentrator nozzle for directional airflow.</p>
<p>Read <a href="/guides/how-to-choose-hair-brush">How to Choose the Right Hair Brush</a> for the full framework and <a href="/guides/best-detangling-brushes">Best Detangling Brushes</a> for an everyday starting point.</p>
<h2>The bottom line</h2>
<p>For thick hair, choose a brush that can work through sections comfortably. Bristle spacing and control matter more than simply buying the largest brush available.</p>
