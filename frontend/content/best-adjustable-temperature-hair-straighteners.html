Compare the settings you can actually select. A broad temperature range can still offer only a few positions. Check the lowest listed setting, the spacing between settings and how you know the tool is ready before choosing. The three current UK models below use genuinely different control schemes: a digital plus/minus scale, an eleven-position scale and a two-position choice.

Quick answer

Pick the control scheme you will use: the Remington S5500 for a digital nine-step scale with a lock, the Cloud Nine Original for eleven positions, or the Cloud Nine Touch for two fixed settings. Check the lowest setting and how the iron shows it is ready before choosing.

Read range, steps and the lowest setting

  • Range: the S5500 spans 150–230°C; the Original spans 100–200°C. Neither range alone says how many choices you get.
  • Positions: nine on the S5500 and eleven on the Original give a stepped scale; the Touch gives two fixed choices.
  • Lowest setting: if you want to start low and only go up if needed, note each model’s bottom figure. Settings are maker figures, not temperatures CombReport has measured at the plate.

Step size is the range divided across the positions, but makers do not always space them evenly, and these listings do not publish the individual steps. If you need a particular figure, check the manual before buying.

Match a control scheme to how you style

  • You like to see a number and lock it: a digital plus/minus scale with a lock, such as the S5500, shows the setting and stops it moving while you style.
  • You want many positions without a display: a stepped scale such as the Original’s gives finer choices; learn how its supplied instructions indicate each one.
  • You use one or two settings anyway: a two-position iron such as the Touch removes choices you would not use.

Before buying, hold a display model and change the setting with the hand you style with. A control you knock by accident, or cannot read without turning the iron, is a reason to look at a lock or a different layout.

Plate format is a separate choice

Controls and plates are listed separately for a reason. The S5500 is sold as a slim straightener with floating plates; the two Cloud Nine irons use a nominal one-inch plate. A setting you like does not make a plate width suit a fringe or a long, dense section, so decide the plate format first if access is the problem, and then pick the control scheme within it.

If you already own an iron with a fixed temperature, the question is whether a specific situation needs a different setting, such as a section that you would rather style at a lower figure. If not, a new control scheme adds buttons without solving anything.
