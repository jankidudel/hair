The useful choice between ghd Air and Helios starts with the part of your drying routine you want to change. A different motor description, nozzle or warranty can be relevant; a newer-looking dryer is not automatically a reason to replace one that already works for you.

Quick answer

Consider Air when its documented concentrator-and-control setup already meets your needs. Consider Helios when its published brushless-motor and contoured-nozzle design is a difference you want to assess. Keep a working dryer if no specific feature addresses an unresolved task.

Ask whether the nozzle changes your working method

If the part you struggle with is directing airflow around a brush, inspect the nozzle and the route your hands take. Can you position it over the section without crowding the brush or collecting neighbouring hair? That is a more useful question than counting technology names on a box.

If possible, examine the tool and controls before buying, with the appliance off. Check where your fingers rest when holding it in the position you actually use. A product photograph can identify the control layout, but it cannot prove that the balance or reach will suit you. Regard these as personal fit checks rather than findings from a hands-on review.

Keep wattage separate from drying results

ghd’s UK wattage support page lists Air at 1800–2100W and Helios at 1850–2200W. These are published input ranges, not a timed comparison or evidence that the higher figure is required for your hair.

Use the hair-dryer wattage guide if the electrical label is the open question. A decision about supply compatibility must come from the exact appliance’s label and instructions, rather than the model name or a general feature table.

The Air product page highlights 2100W, while the support page gives a range. Keep those statements attached to their sources rather than presenting them as two competing performance measurements. If a retailer publishes a different figure, first check the market and model reference. Do not average different figures or use the largest one as a reason to buy. The appliance supplied to you still needs its own label and compatible supply.

Check the attachment you would actually use

Before choosing a bundle, list the attachment needed for your routine and verify whether it is included in that exact listing. A compatible accessory can still be sold separately, and an image beside a dryer is not a complete box-contents statement.

If a diffuser is essential, use the diffuser compatibility guide to organise the fit check. Do not assume that an attachment for another ghd dryer, generation or kit transfers to the model you are buying. Confirm the model-specific source instead of relying on brand identity alone.

Make the replacement case concrete

Write one sentence describing what your present dryer cannot do conveniently. Then identify the published difference that could address that task. If the sentence is only “I want a better dryer”, return to nozzle access, controls, attachment fit or ownership terms and make the requirement more specific.

The general hair-dryer selection guide covers the broader format decision. This comparison is useful once Air and Helios are the actual candidates. It does not establish that either produces a faster, quieter or shinier result in your routine.

FAQ

Is Helios automatically the better purchase?

No. Choose a documented difference that matters to your routine; the page does not provide an independent performance ranking.

Does the higher wattage range prove faster drying?

No. Published electrical input is not a timed result on your hair.

Are attachments automatically interchangeable?

Do not assume so. Verify the exact dryer, accessory and box contents before buying.
