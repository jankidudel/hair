The D38 combines D3-style pins with a wider, air-cushioned paddle body. That changes the head and section-handling question: it is not simply another row-count version of the seven-row D3.

Quick answer

Consider D3 when its styling-head format suits the local section you control. Consider D38 when the broader cushioned paddle serves a repeated task. If a brush already handles that section comfortably, shared pin language alone supplies little reason to add another. The main choice is head construction and access.

Compare the head construction

Denman describes D38 through its relationship to D3-style pins and its air-cushioned paddle format. D3 is the seven-row Original Styler. A shared pin description identifies one part of the construction. It does not make the complete heads identical. Match the head to the section

A narrower styling head can be considered for a local section where access matters. A wider paddle raises a separate question about the amount of hair presented and your ability to steer the head. Those are editorial handling interpretations. Try the movement against the section you actually want to control before regarding a broader outline as a reason to increase section size.

Why shared pins do not settle handling

Pins interact with the pad, head shape, grip and the section placed on the brush. The D38’s staggered arrangement and air-cushioned description belong to its named construction. They establish neither a universal detangling result nor a timed advantage over D3. A similar pin reference cannot approve replacement-pad interchangeability. Keep an exact spare code and compatible-body statement beside any replacement order.

Identify the task before another brush

Begin with what you want the brush to do, such as detangling a prepared section or guiding a styling movement. A named brush may appear in more than one routine without making those jobs identical. The curl-defining and detangling format guide helps separate those tasks. Choose the permitted starting state and handling method from the exact product’s directions.

Check what the current brush leaves unfinished

If an existing brush already lets you reach, guide and release the section, another head may repeat the task. A paddle becomes worth comparing when a broader surface changes the handling you want. Before buying, compare the actual head and grip in the orientation you use. Working dimensions, comparative speed and finish retention remain unmeasured, so they cannot settle the choice here.
