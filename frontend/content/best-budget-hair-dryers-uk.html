Quick answer

Start with the feature you would miss on wash day. Compare a basic concentrator dryer, a dryer with its diffuser supplied and a compact model if storage is the constraint. If you would buy an attachment separately, include that cost when working out the real price.

Three exact UK listings cover those jobs. The Remington Power Dry D3010 has 3 heat and 2 speed settings, a cool shot and a concentrator. The Remington Shine Therapy D5216 adds a diffuser in the box, with the same 3 heat and 2 speed layout and a removable rear grille. The Remington On The Go D1500 has a folding handle, 2 heat and speed settings, a concentrator and a compact diffuser, but no cool shot is listed.

Count the attachment you would buy later

The cheapest dryer is not always the cheapest routine. If you diffuse curls most wash days, a dryer without a diffuser in the box means finding and paying for one that fits that exact model. In this shortlist, the D3010 lists only a concentrator, while the D5216 and D1500 both list a diffuser. For a curly routine, compare the D5216’s price with the D3010 plus a separately bought diffuser, and remember that fit has to be confirmed by model.

Worked example: a reader with fine, straight hair dries in ten minutes with a concentrator and keeps the dryer on a hook. The D3010’s settings, cool shot and hang-up loop cover that routine. A second reader has shoulder-length curls and diffuses three times a week. The D5216 includes a diffuser, so there is no extra part to buy.

When storage is the constraint

The D1500 is the compact option: Remington lists 172 × 85 × 210 mm, a folding handle and a 1.8 m cord, against 205.5 × 111.5 × 277 mm for the D3010. The trade-off is fewer settings and no listed cool shot. A shorter cord also matters if your socket is not near a mirror. If you need a dryer for international trips, voltage is a separate question covered in our travel dryer guide.
