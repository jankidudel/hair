There is no single drying time for every routine. Start with whether the dryer has always taken this long or became slower recently. If it has always felt slow, compare starting moisture, section size and the attachment on your next ordinary session. If airflow changed suddenly, check the accessible intake and follow your model’s manual.

  • It has always taken this long: compare your routine across two sessions, then tighten sections with the blow-dry routine.
  • It became slower recently: check the intake and the model’s filter instructions.

Wattage on the label cannot predict your drying time, and this page gives no minute range: length, density, starting moisture and attachment all change it.

First checks

  1. Compare the dryer with and without an attachment only as the manual allows.
  2. Notice whether airflow changed suddenly or has always felt similar.
  3. Check the accessible intake for visible lint after switching off, unplugging, and cooling.

Always slow: make a repeatable reader comparison

This is a suggested self-check, not a Comb Report test. On two ordinary sessions, note how damp the hair feels at the start, section size, attachment, airflow setting, and when each section seems dry. Keep most factors consistent and vary one. Length, density, product, and room conditions can alter the comparison.

Slower recently: use the right evidence for filter care

If airflow dropped suddenly across every section, switch off, unplug and let the dryer cool, then look at the accessible intake. Some dryers have removable filter cages and others do not. A Dyson Supersonic Professional HD18 guide, for example, connects reduced airflow with its model-specific filter procedure. Do not copy those removal steps to a different dryer; find that dryer’s exact manufacturer guide.

Know when to stop

Sparks, unusual smells, a damaged cable or plug, repeated shutoff, or a loose cover are reasons to stop using the appliance and contact the manufacturer. Do not open or repair internal parts. If routine changes improve a historically slow session, a replacement may be unnecessary.

Separate hair and routine from device behaviour

Dense or long hair can contain more sections to work through, so elapsed time alone does not tell you whether the dryer is weak. Compare the same section on successive days, keeping starting moisture and attachment similar. If the air feels unchanged but you need fewer passes with smaller sections, the routine was probably the useful place to adjust. If airflow itself has suddenly dropped across every section, shift attention to the accessible intake and model instructions. If the air is moving normally but stays cold at every heat setting, that is a different problem: see hair dryer only blows cold air.

Attachments and airflow are a trade-off

A concentrator directs air into a smaller area, which can help with a brush-led finish but may make a broad rough dry feel slower. A diffuser distributes air differently and is chosen for a different styling goal. Compare the attachment against the result you want, then check whether it is fitted correctly. Avoid assuming the highest heat or a wattage number always produces the quickest satisfactory finish.
