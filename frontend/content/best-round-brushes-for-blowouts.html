Quick answer

For a fringe or crop, start with a small round brush that can turn under the section. For a bob, choose a middle size that gives the ends a bend while leaving room near the jaw. For longer layers, try a wider barrel if you mainly want a loose curve and can keep the section under control.

Round brush shortlist by job

Job UK examples Fit to check
Reach a fringe or short layer ghd Ceramic Radial Size 1, 25 mm, or Denman Head Hugger Extra Small, 25 mm. Can the section make one controlled turn and release without riding over the ends of the brush?
Turn the ends of a bob ghd Ceramic Radial Size 2, 35 mm, or Denman Head Hugger Small, 33 mm. Is there room to turn your wrist near the jaw? Move down one size if the head crowds the section.
Shape long layers with a loose bend ghd Ceramic Radial Size 3, 45 mm, or Denman Head Hugger Medium, 43 mm. Does your normal section lie across the barrel? Step up only if you can keep the ends from slipping free.

The ghd and Denman numbers are published barrel diameters. The bristles make the working head wider. Denman’s Head Hugger has a shaped barrel, so check the surface you will actually use instead of assuming it feels the same as a straight cylinder.

US alternative

Olivia Garden’s Ceramic + Ion Thermal Round range comes in several barrel sizes. Read the exact US size on the listing and check the bristle-to-bristle clearance if your shortest layer is the main reason you are buying. The brand uses its own size codes across the range.

Make a three-line shortlist

Write down “fringe”, “ends” and “long layers”. Next to each, mark whether you style it regularly and whether your current brush can release that section easily. Choose for the job where you regularly lose control. If the fringe and the length need very different head sizes, one brush may cover the main blowout while a second smaller brush handles the fringe.

Use the round brush size guide to check dimensions. For technique, see blow-drying a fringe, blow-drying a bob or blow-drying long hair.

Plan the three sections you use at home

Say you have a jaw-length bob with a fringe and want to turn the ends in. Compare a middle barrel for the ends, then hold a brush of similar size near the fringe to judge the fit. If there is enough room to lift and release the fringe, try one brush for both jobs. If the handle runs out of room near the jaw or the head crowds the front, a smaller brush may make the difference. Give the part you style every week more weight than a finish you only do occasionally.

For long layers, use the size table as a starting point and check the amount of hair you actually pick up. Hold the brush where you normally turn the ends and notice how much of the head the section covers. If the section tends to fall off the side, try adjusting your section before buying a wider barrel. If a brush is only awkward when you hold it with a dryer in the other hand, also check handle length and where you naturally grip it.

What to compare on a retailer page

Find the listed barrel size next to the exact variant. If the retailer gives the brush’s total width as well, use that to check clearance around short hair. Then check the bristle shape and handle using the product images. A colour name or size number alone gives you too little information to compare brushes across brands.
