Quick answer

Compare the regular standard and Naturally Curly brushes by their documented teeth and the detangling job you repeat. If your usual brush already moves through manageable sections comfortably, use that as your starting point.

Tangle Teezer describes the regular Ultimate Detangler as a handled brush for wet hair and the shower, with two-tiered teeth: long teeth detangle and shorter teeth smooth. It describes the Naturally Curly version as having longer, firmer teeth with shorter teeth alongside, designed for wet hair and positioned for 3C to 4C hair types. The useful question is not which brush is better, but which tooth description matches the sections you detangle every wash day.

Hair-type labels are the maker’s guidance, not a fit test. Use them to narrow the shortlist, then decide with the section you actually detangle.

Match the brush to the detangling job

Picture one wash day. If you apply conditioner, divide hair into a few sections and work from the ends upward, the brush has to move through each section without you changing grip. A reader whose sections are loose waves or looser curls may find the regular two-tier layout covers that job, because the long teeth separate and the short teeth follow. A reader with tightly coiled sections that hold their shape may lean towards the Naturally Curly description, because Tangle Teezer pairs its longer, firmer teeth with that hair type.

Section size matters as much as the brush. If you take large sections because your hair is long, Tangle Teezer suggests the Large standard brush rather than the regular one. That size question has its own comparison in Ultimate Detangler Large versus Regular. If your hair is fine and you are choosing between softer options, the Ultimate Detangler versus Fine & Fragile comparison covers that pair.

When to check another size or format

Check another option when the brush you own already reaches the end of a section comfortably but you want a different finish. Detangling and curl definition are different tasks: once sections are detangled, a defining brush or your fingers take over. Our curly hair brush guide separates those steps.

Two worked cases help. First, a reader with shoulder-length 3A curls who already uses the regular brush in the shower and finishes in a few minutes has no reason in these listings to switch. Second, a reader with 4B coils who finds the regular brush needs several passes per section could compare the Naturally Curly description, then check its availability, because the listing was shown as coming soon on the date we checked.

Care is the same for both: remove hair after use, rinse occasionally with warm water and a little mild soap, and let the brush air dry. Keep both away from dryers and hot tools.
