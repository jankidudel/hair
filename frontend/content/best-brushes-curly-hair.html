Curly hair routines vary, so the best brush is not a universal answer. Start by deciding when you detangle and what finish you want afterwards.

Detangling during a damp routine

Many people prefer open-spaced combs or flexible brushes when hair is damp and conditioned according to their normal routine. The goal is control: take sections, begin at the ends and work upward rather than pulling through the whole length at once.

Dry brushing changes the finish

Brushing dry curls can create a softer, larger or less-defined appearance. That may be exactly what you want, but it is worth recognising that the brush is changing the style rather than merely removing tangles.

What to look for

Choose a tool with spacing, flexibility and a size that suits your hair density. A comfortable handle matters when you work in sections. If you use a brush in the shower, verify that it is intended for wet use and clean it regularly.

For tool choices, compare Best Detangling Brushes with Wide-Tooth Comb vs Detangling Brush. For wash-day drying, see Best Hair Dryers for Curly Hair.

The bottom line

Choose the brush or comb that fits your routine and preferred finish. Gentle sectioning is more important than finding a supposed miracle tool.
