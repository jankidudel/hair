A crease usually starts where the clamp presses a folded or uneven section against the barrel. Work with a section that sits flat, align the ends before closing the clamp, and move as the manufacturer directs rather than pinning one fold in place. A wand changes the handling because it has no clamp, but it brings its own contact and grip considerations.

If residue is visible, clean the tool only as its exact manual permits; use How to clean a curling iron or wand. Stop if the clamp edge, hinge or barrel is damaged.

First checks

  1. Check the iron’s instructions for clamp direction and hair preparation.
  2. Take a narrow, detangled section and align the ends.
  3. Practice the placement with the tool off and cool before using it.

One illustrated handling sequence

With the iron off and cool, hold a narrow section, place the aligned ends under the clamp as the model guide shows, close without folding them, and rehearse a smooth roll and release. When styling, follow its manual for direction and settings. Let the curl cool before assessing the shape. If a crease appears, note its location before changing one placement step.

Compare tool formats by handling

A clamped iron holds the section but can mark a folded end. A wand removes the clamp yet requires guiding hair around an exposed barrel according to its instructions. An automatic curler has a different feed and release mechanism; follow its own guide and do not infer it will eliminate every crease.

Avoid a forced fix

A different barrel material does not guarantee no clamp mark. Do not prescribe one heat setting or holding time for unspecified devices. If the clamp sticks or its edge is damaged, stop using it and seek manufacturer support.

Use the mark location as feedback

A dent at the tips often suggests the ends were folded when the clamp closed. A mark halfway up the curl can reflect a section that bunched during rolling. A line that appears on every curl at the same point calls for checking the clamp and barrel after the tool cools. The useful correction depends on that location; doing more passes over the same mark may add handling without fixing the placement.

Rehearse a release path

On a cool unplugged iron, practice closing the clamp on a flat ribbon of hair, rolling as the manual describes, and opening it before the section is tugged. The movement should let the hair leave the tool rather than dragging through a closed clamp. When using the heated tool, keep your hands clear of hot surfaces and follow its model-specific instructions. If a strand catches despite a clear path, stop and inspect the cool tool for damage.
