The Denman D3 shapes a section through rows of pins. The Bounce Curl Define EdgeLift adds a shaped edge to split and guide the section. Choose the movement you can repeat with the curl group you want. Both are styling brushes for detangled hair with product applied; the difference is which part of the brush does the shaping.

Quick answer

Choose the D3 if you like brushing a section flat through rows of pins and want a brush you can take apart for cleaning. Choose the Define EdgeLift if you prefer tilting the brush so hair falls into its grooves while the edge splits the section, and if you want a top edge for shorter pieces.

Change the section, not just the brush

The step that most changes the outcome is how wide the section is and which way you hold the brush. Try two ordinary example sections on product-coated, detangled hair:

  1. A wide section near the crown. With the D3, lay the section across the rows and brush down the length; the pins shape the whole width at once. With the Define EdgeLift, tilt the brush so hair drops into the grooves and let the side edge split it as you glide down.
  2. A narrow section at the front or a short layer. The D3 can be turned on its side to cover less width. The Define EdgeLift’s top edge is described for exactly this job: bangs, layers and shorter pieces.

If you prefer fewer, larger curl groups, try wider sections first; if you want smaller groups, narrow the section before changing the tool. Scrunch, then diffuse or air-dry as you normally do.

Care and replacement parts

Denman’s D3 instructions describe a full strip-down: slide out the pad, remove the pin strips, wash in warm soapy water, rinse, dry and reassemble with the shorter rows on the outside. That makes the D3 the easier of the two to clean thoroughly. Bounce Curl describes removing hair by hand or with a cleaning comb, washing with a mild shampoo and air-drying with the bristles facing down.
