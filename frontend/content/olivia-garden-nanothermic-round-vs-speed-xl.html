Olivia Garden’s US charts give two widths for a NanoThermic round brush. The barrel diameter measures the core. The bristle figure measures across the outside of the bristles. Compare each measurement with the corresponding one on the Speed XL chart. Done that way, the NT and NTXL rows with the same number line up exactly: both charts give the same inch figures for each pair. The difference Olivia Garden names is that the Speed XL is extra-long, and the chart doesn’t turn that into a length you can compare.

What changes with the Speed XL barrel

On the charts, the paired diameters are the same. What Olivia Garden calls out is the extra-long Speed XL design. Without a stated length for each size, you can’t tell from the chart how much longer an NTXL brush is than its NT partner, so read “XL” as the maker’s description rather than a measurement.

The Speed XL chart also gives alternate nominal metric labels. NTXL-34, for example, is labelled 1¼″ and 34 mm at the barrel. 1¼″ converts to 31.75 mm, so the 34 mm label is the maker’s nominal size, not a conversion. Keep the two apart. Measure a brush you own

Measure across the solid barrel where the bristles part, then across the outside of the bristle tips at the widest point. Write both down. The round brush size guide shows which part each figure describes.

Worked case: a brush with a 2″ bristle envelope. Your brush measures about 2 inches across the bristle tips. On both charts, that is the bristle figure for NT-34 and NTXL-34. Check the core too: both list 1¼″.

If a listing gives only one number, ask the seller whether it is the core or the bristle tips, or check the model code against the chart.

Worked case: a 34 mm label and a measured 1¼″. A listing says “34 mm” and you measure 1¼ inches (31.75 mm) across the core of an NTXL-34. Both can be right: 34 mm is the maker’s nominal label and 31.75 mm is the conversion of the 1¼″ chart figure. Compare core with core, and bristle tips with bristle tips, using one basis at a time.
