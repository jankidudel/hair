Cleaning a hairbrush means removing trapped hair, then lifting residue with a method that suits how the brush is built. The construction decides the method: a one-piece plastic detangler, a brush with a removable pad and a natural-bristle cushion brush each need different care.

Cleaning a comb instead? Use how to clean a hair comb for the teeth, spine and material checks. A brush’s cushion or bristle-cleaning method is not automatically a comb-cleaning method.

Quick answer

Remove hair first, then clean with as little moisture as the brush's construction allows, and let it dry fully before use or storage. Find your brush in the table below: a named model's directions override the general rule. Powered brushes are not cleaned this way: an electrical smoothing brush needs straightening-brush care, and an airflow brush has its own hot-air-brush cleaning method.

Remove trapped hair

Lift loose hair with your fingers or a maker-recommended cleaning tool, working gently from the base of the bristles or pins outward. Do not drive pointed objects into a cushion or use the base as a lever. If a bundle resists, separate it into smaller areas. Put the removed hair in the bin rather than rinsing it into the sink. Doing this whenever hair collects makes the fuller clean much simpler.

Clean residue between the pins

Tangle Teezer example. For the US Ultimate Detangler Fine & Fragile, Tangle Teezer gives a rinse and mild-soap method for buildup. Its generic care copy and FAQ give different drying orientations, so if the instructions supplied with your exact brush leave this unclear, ask the maker.

Denman D3 example. Denman UK's D3 deep-clean directions slide the pad up out of the handle, open it and remove each strip of pins by its bobble end. Scrub the empty pad, the pin rows and the handle in warm soapy water, rinse everything thoroughly and dry each part. Replace the pins with the bobbles at the bottom end, the two shorter rows on the outside, then slide the pad back in with the crowned D at the top and leave it to dry. That permission belongs to the D3; see the UK D3 listing for the model itself.

Any other brush. A soft cloth lightly dampened with warm water, or a soft toothbrush kept only for the brush, can lift surface residue around the handle, back and bristle base. Work around the bristles rather than scrubbing them. Avoid cleaners that are not recommended for the finish. Stubborn residue may need several gentle passes rather than force.

Dry it before use or storage

Blot visible moisture with a clean towel, then leave the brush in an airy place rather than putting it straight into a drawer. Positioning the bristles downward can help water leave the base, provided it does not bend them. Use or store the brush only when it is dry to the touch, with no moisture held near a cushion, joins or the handle.

How often, and what to check next

Remove hair as it collects, wipe the brush when residue is visible, and do a fuller clean when the base feels crowded. The right frequency depends on how often you use the brush and the products in your styling routine.

Grey or white lint is usually ordinary buildup; see what grey fuzz on a hairbrush usually is. If bristles are bent, missing or uncomfortable after a suitable clean, or an odour remains after complete drying, use the condition-based replacement guide.
