First locate the unwanted line: at an end, part-way along a curl or repeatedly at the same contact point. Review how that section entered and left the clamp before changing the tool or adding another heated pass. Appearance alone does not establish one cause, and an intended wave pattern is a different result.

Quick answer

Check the prepared section, its position under the clamp and the exact model's release action. Rehearse the movement with the appliance cool and unplugged, then follow its actual operating instructions. If the edge, hinge or barrel is rough, damaged or unstable, stop using it instead of trying to style around the fault.

Record the point where the mark appears

Look at one manageable section and note whether the line is visible immediately after release or appears during later arranging. Keep the original section boundary and movement in mind. A single observation is useful for planning a check, but it is not a controlled test of the appliance or proof that heat is the cause.

Use these as separate next checks, rather than a chart assigning a definite cause to each mark.

Follow the actual clamp sequence

The US Conair CD851 manual documents a clamp curling iron. Use the manual for the model you hold to establish placement, the permitted turn and release. A clipless-wand guide cannot establish those clamp actions.

Some tools' methods start differently along the section. Do not impose one universal ends-first instruction or copy the roll from another mechanism. Clamp-iron use keeps a named operating example separate from a general troubleshooting prompt.

Rehearse a clear entry and exit

With the tool cool and unplugged, isolate a section and consider where it lies as the clamp closes. Keep loose neighbouring hair out of the path and look for an end folding beneath another part of the section. Understand how to release the clamp before the hair is pulled against it.

The rehearsal should clarify the movement, not demonstrate a heated result or authorise a new contact time. If the documented action is difficult to perform at that location, reconsider the section or format before switching on. Do not use repeated heated attempts to investigate a part that catches while cool.

Separate residue, damage and placement

Visible residue belongs to curling-iron care, using only the exact model's permitted method. A clean surface does not correct a bent clamp or prove that the section is placed well. A rough edge, damaged finish or loose mechanism needs a stop-use response.

If the tool is in normal condition and the mark appears at release, review one placement choice on the next suitable session. Do not change several products, the whole sectioning plan and the appliance settings together and then assign the outcome to one factor.

Change formats only for a defined job

If you dislike the clamp's handling even when following its instructions, clamp versus wand explains the alternative holding action. A wand brings its own exposed-barrel and hand-position requirements; it is not a guaranteed crease-free result.

For a deliberately repeated press pattern, use waver versus curling iron. For a smooth shape that forms and later relaxes, use curl-retention troubleshooting. Those owners answer different questions from an unwanted line at clamp release.
