Cleaning a hair comb means reaching the spaces between the teeth without damaging their edges. A comb with a smooth one-piece body, a wooden handle or a metal tail may need different care, even when each is sold for everyday styling.

Identify the comb before choosing a method

Look for the maker’s material description and aftercare instructions. Check whether the comb has a coating, join, insert, attached handle or case. A material name alone does not establish soaking, heat or chemical resistance.

Remove loose hair first so you can inspect the teeth. Set aside a comb with a cracked tip, sharp seam, splinter or loose part. Cleaning removes residue; it does not repair the edge that contacts your hair.

A named example that does not start with soaking

Kent’s handmade-comb aftercare describes wiping with a cloth, using a soft toothbrush on the teeth and allowing the comb to dry away from heat and direct sunlight. Those directions do not provide a universal soaking recipe.

Use the aftercare section for the actual product family. Instructions for cleaning the bristles of a wooden hairbrush on the same page are not instructions for washing a comb.

Work from loose debris to the tooth bases

  1. Remove loose hair. Lift strands away with your fingers, taking care around a pointed tail. Do not drag a metal blade between the teeth.
  2. Support the spine. Hold the comb so a thin tooth is not taking the force of the clean. Use good light to see the spaces at the bases.
  3. Wipe the accessible surfaces. Use the cloth and moisture level permitted by the maker. Do not add a solvent because a mark looks stubborn.
  4. Clean between teeth with a permitted soft tool. For a comb whose care allows it, use a soft brush to lift debris from each side. Avoid levering the teeth apart or forcing a tool into a narrow gap.
  5. Use a wet wash only when permitted. Follow the exact detergent, rinse and drying requirements. Do not boil the comb or put it in a dishwasher as a general household shortcut.
  6. Dry before closing the case. Follow the product’s drying instructions and check joins and tooth bases. Do not use a hot hair dryer or radiator to speed the process.
  7. Inspect again. Look for a rough edge or crack revealed when residue is gone. Stop using a damaged comb rather than sanding or reshaping a tooth without manufacturer guidance.

Ordinary cleaning is not disinfection

This page covers personal household care. It does not provide a salon disinfection protocol or claim that wiping removes every microorganism. If a professional or shared-use setting requires a specific hygiene process, use the applicable requirements and a method compatible with the exact tool.

For a brush with pins, bristles or a cushion, use how to clean a hair brush. If a cleaned comb still catches, read when to replace a hairbrush or comb before returning it to the routine.
