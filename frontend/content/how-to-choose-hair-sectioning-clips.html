Quick answer: a sectioning clip temporarily parks hair while you work elsewhere. Choose a jaw that reaches around the section and holds it without repeated re-clipping. A decorative claw clip has a different job: holding a finished arrangement.

Compare the mechanism in your hand

An alligator-style clip has a hinged jaw and often teeth along the gripping edges; a duckbill-style clip is long and narrow, useful for laying a flatter, smaller section away from the work area. Names vary by retailer, so inspect the actual opening, jaw length, teeth or padding, spring and contact surface. A large clip can still slide on a small section; a short narrow clip can be overloaded by a dense bundle.

Test placement before replacing a clip

Make the working section narrower and park the unused hair away from the tool's path. Try holding a folded section rather than a loose sheet. Release and reposition if a clip leaves an unwanted bend; no design promises a mark-free result. Clean residue from the contact edges according to the clip maker, and inspect the spring and teeth when hold changes. Do not place ordinary clips against heated parts unless the exact model permits it.

Build a practical small set

Start with the number of sections your routine actually uses, not a salon-sized pack. A small lower section and an unused upper layer may require only two or three clips. Choose one that closes on the smallest bundle you regularly park and one with enough opening for a broader layer, if a single design cannot do both. Test placement with the styling appliance switched off so you can see whether the jaw sits clear of the working path. If a clip repeatedly releases, compare the section size and jaw contact before blaming spring strength. Teeth that meet hair at an angle may hold differently from a flat contact surface; there is no guaranteed winner for every strand type.

Choose the mechanism that addresses your actual issue: opening for a broad bundle, contact for a slipping section, or a flatter surface when a visible bend is inconvenient. Verify exact cleaning and heat restrictions before use near an appliance. A clip that holds your working sections already needs no upgrade.

Separate a preparation clip from a roller-holding clip

When setting a few self-grip rollers, first use a temporary clip to park unused hair. Then check whether the roller's instructions require a different clip to hold the wrapped section. The Beauty Works Bouncy Blowout set explicitly includes sectioning clips and tells users to secure its placed rollers with them. That permission belongs to this kit; a loose clip from another pack should be checked for fit and any dryer-heat restrictions before it enters the routine.

For a continuous heatless ribbon, a temporary clip may hold the rod at the crown while both sides are wrapped. The Kitsch set directions show a claw clip for that positioning step and say it is not included. The end scrunchies have a separate fastening job. A wide jaw useful for parking a broad section may be clumsy on a narrow roller, while a small roller clip may be inadequate at the crown. Before buying, map the actual stages and count the clips each stage needs.
