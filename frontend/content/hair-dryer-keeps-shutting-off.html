Quick answer

First check which part of the session stops. A dryer that switches off completely, one that keeps blowing cold air and one that pauses in a documented mode have different next steps. If there is damage, a burning smell or repeated unexplained shutdown, stop using the dryer and contact the maker.

Many dryers protect themselves when air cannot move freely. Electrical Safety First advises keeping air openings clear and following the maker’s filter instructions. A retailer support page for a BaByliss dryer gives the same first checks for a dryer that turns off during use: unplug, keep vents uncovered, clean the rear filter, remove hair build-up and let it cool fully before restarting.

A documented pause is not a fault

Some dryers are designed to reduce heat when you stop. Dyson’s Supersonic Nural HD16 manual describes Pause detect: when the dryer is put down, the heater switches off, airflow and noise drop, and the capsule light pulses blue. Pick it up and it resumes. If your dryer has a feature like this, the “cutting out” you notice may be the dryer doing what its manual describes.

The same manual, and the HD07 manual for the original Supersonic, explain that the LEDs flash continuously when the machine needs attention: clean the filter, and if all three red lights continue to flash, contact the Dyson Helpline and do not turn it back on. For the exact filter steps, see how to clean a Dyson Supersonic filter by model.

Check the filter and vents first

A clogged intake is the most common ordinary cause of a dryer stopping mid-session. Unplug the dryer and let it cool. Remove only the parts your manual says are removable, clear lint and product residue, and refit them as directed. The hair dryer filter cleaning guide covers the general sequence. Do not cover the intake with your hand or a towel while drying.

Worked example: your dryer stops about ten minutes into drying long hair, and starts again after a few minutes. You unplug it, find a mat of lint on the rear grille, clean it as the manual directs and dry again with the intake clear. If it now runs through the session, you have your answer. If it stops again with a clean filter, stop there and contact the maker.

When to stop using it

Electrical Safety First says to unplug a hair dryer and contact the retailer or manufacturer if you notice an odd smell, sparking, cracks or damage, burn marks or buzzing sounds. Do not open the casing, bypass a safety feature or keep restarting it to get through a session. Register your dryer with the maker so you can be contacted about recalls.
