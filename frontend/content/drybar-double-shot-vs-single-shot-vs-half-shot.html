Quick answer

Start with Half Shot if a fringe or short layer is the main section giving you trouble. Choose Single Shot if you want a full-size round brush for a bob or layered blowout. Choose Double Shot if you mainly want to move a longer section over a broad oval head and have room to turn it.

Brush Maker-listed head A useful fit check
Half Shot 1.5-inch round barrel. Check if you can start and release a fringe or a short face-framing piece without winding it around the head twice.
Single Shot 2.25-inch round barrel. Check if the ends of your bob reach far enough to bend over the brush, with room to turn near the jaw.
Double Shot 2.4-inch oval barrel. Check whether a longer section lays across the broad face while leaving space around your shortest layer.

Drybar’s figures describe the named barrels. For storage or clearance, allow extra room for bristles and the shape of the handle. Stock may vary by finish and retailer. Match the brush name and barrel to the box before ordering.

Choose for the section you style most often

A long haircut can still have pieces that are too short for the brush you use on the back. If those pieces take up most of your styling time, give them more weight than the haircut’s overall length. If you mainly shape long lengths and only smooth the fringe with a separate tool, compare the Single and Double Shot first.

With the brush cool and unplugged, rehearse one turn and release on a small section. If you need to bend the wrist sharply or the ends wind around again, a smaller head may be simpler. If every long section needs to be divided again, see whether a larger head gives you more usable surface.

Compare the brush to the longest part of your routine

Consider a shoulder-length bob with short face-framing pieces. If you want one brush for the full blowout and the outside sections take most of the time, start with the Single Shot and check the front for clearance before ordering. If you usually wear the lengths as they dry and only shape the pieces in front, start with the Half Shot. If you are shopping for longer layers and mainly want a polished bend at the ends, the Double Shot’s oval shape gives you another surface to compare.

You can separate two common fit problems by rehearsing with the cool, unplugged brush. First, see whether the section reaches far enough across the head to hold it steady. Then see whether you can move the head away without winding up the ends again. If you lose your grip at the first step, compare a shape with a more manageable working surface. If the release is awkward, compare the smaller head and a smaller section.

Read the product title before the colour

Retailers may place multiple Shot tools on the same page, and a similar finish does not identify the barrel. Check the chosen variant after selecting a colour. Make a note of the model name, the barrel shape and the stated size, then compare that combination across sellers. If you already have an oval dryer brush that handles the lengths, compare only the two round sizes for the part of your routine the oval cannot reach.
