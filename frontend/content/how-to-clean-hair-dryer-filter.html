The filter or air intake is usually the vented cover near the back of a hair dryer. It draws air into the tool, so it can gather lint, dust and loose hair over time. A short, dry clean of the accessible surface keeps the area easier to inspect and is a sensible part of looking after an electrical styling tool.

The exact design varies. Some dryers have a removable rear cover, some have a mesh surface that stays in place and others use a filter system with specific instructions. Always check the guide for your model before removing a cover or using water. This page is about routine external care, not taking a dryer apart or trying to repair it.

What you need

Start with a clean, dry soft brush and a dry microfiber or soft cloth. A small soft brush can lift loose surface debris without pushing it into the intake. Avoid sharp tools, metal objects, sprays and liquids around the body or vent. If your model has a removable filter, use only the cleaning method specified by the manufacturer.

Step 1: switch off, unplug and cool the dryer

Switch the dryer off, unplug it from the socket and give it time to cool completely. Place it on a stable surface with good light so you can see the rear intake and any lint around the grille. Do not clean while it is warm, connected to power or sitting near a sink.

Step 2: identify the filter or intake design

Look at the rear of the dryer and check the product instructions before moving anything. A fixed mesh should generally be cleaned in place from the outside. A removable cover should come off only by the method shown for that model. If the cover does not release easily, do not force it: the instructions are more reliable than a generic technique.

If the appliance is a two-arm drying straightener, use wet-to-dry straightener care rather than borrowing this dryer's access method. A similar mesh pattern does not establish the same release, cloth or washing permission. Match the filter and appliance revision before acting, particularly when an older manual illustrates a different removable part.

Step 3: remove visible lint and dust gently

Use the dry soft brush to lift lint from the outside of the grille or filter. Brush away from openings where possible, using short light strokes. Wipe the surrounding exterior with a dry cloth. The aim is to remove what you can see, not to push a tool deep into the intake or dislodge internal parts.

If the manual says a detachable filter can be cleaned with water, follow its instructions exactly and allow it to dry fully before refitting it. Do not assume a removable-looking cover is washable. The main dryer body, cord and electrical connections should remain dry.

Step 4: refit the cover securely

If you removed a cover or filter as instructed, check that it is completely dry and seated properly before using the dryer again. Do not run the tool with a loose cover or a missing part. Keep the cord and plug away from moisture while you put the dryer back in its usual storage place.

How often should you check it?

There is no universal timetable because storage, household dust and frequency of use vary. Check when you notice visible lint, after a period of frequent styling or before packing the dryer away for travel. A brief check every few uses is easier than letting debris build up until the intake is difficult to inspect. If you use a hot-air brush too, the same dry, unplugged approach applies to its accessible vents; read our hot-air brush cleaning guide.

Store the dryer where the intake stays visible

After it has cooled, store the dryer where the rear intake is not pressed tightly into fabric, loose drawer debris or a crowded travel pouch. This is not a special storage system; it simply makes the next quick visual check easier. Before using a stored dryer again, glance at the cord, plug, cover and attachment fit as well as the intake. If anything looks damaged or insecure, use the manufacturer support guidance instead of trying to correct it yourself.

What cleaning can and cannot do

Routine external care can help with It is not a solution for
Removing visible lint and dust from accessible areas Opening the appliance or repairing internal components
Keeping a removable cover clean when the instructions allow it A damaged cable, plug, cover or attachment
Making the air intake easier to inspect Unusual smells, sparking, faults or a dryer that will not operate normally

Stop using the dryer and follow the manufacturer's support guidance if you notice damaged parts, an unusual smell, sparking, a loose cable, excessive heat or a change in normal operation. Cleaning is not a repair method and is not a substitute for the safety instructions supplied with the appliance.

The bottom line

Clean the accessible filter or air intake only when the dryer is unplugged, cool and dry. Use a soft dry brush for visible debris, follow the maker's instructions for removable parts and never force a cover. When cleaning does not restore airflow

After a permitted exterior or removable-filter clean, compare the airflow with the tool’s normal behaviour. If it remains unusually weak, repeatedly shuts off, or smells unusual, stop using it and contact the maker. A filter clean cannot establish that internal components are sound; do not disassemble the dryer to investigate. For a focused next step, see the slow-dryer troubleshooting.
