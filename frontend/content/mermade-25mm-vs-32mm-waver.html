Quick answer

Start with the shortest section you want to wave. Compare the 25 mm Mini and 32 mm Original by the room you have to place the clamp and the wave scale you want. Check the exact UK listing before assuming other Mermade wavers share the same design.

Mermade says the 25mm barrels create a tighter, smaller wave than the 32mm, and the 32mm creates a larger, undone wave. Both pink Pro listings name the same supporting details: multiple heat settings, a one-hour auto shut-off, a 2.5m swivel cord, universal voltage and a two-year warranty. So the choice is about wave scale and whether the head can sit where you need it.

Barrel diameter is not the whole head

The 25mm and 32mm figures describe the barrels. A triple-barrel waver also has a top plate, a hinge and a handle, and those set how close to the root you can place it. Mermade does not publish the height or depth of either head, so we cannot say from the listings how much shorter a layer the 25mm can reach. A smaller barrel does not prove the whole head is smaller by the same amount.

That is why the most useful check is physical. With the waver unplugged and completely cool, close it gently on your shortest visible layer and see whether the section sits across all three barrels with room to clamp. If the ends slip out before the clamp closes, that size will be awkward on that layer.

The “longer sections” row is arithmetic, not a test: a smaller wave repeats more often along the same length, so expect more clamping steps to cover it.

Work through your shortest and longest sections

Short-layer example: you have face-framing layers that end around the chin and want them to match the rest. Mermade’s directions start at the top, close to the roots, and move down. On a short layer you only get a few clamps before the ends, so the size whose head you can place cleanly near the root matters more than the wave scale. Do the cool test on that layer first.

Longer-section example: your hair falls past your shoulders and you want a soft, loose texture. The 32mm’s larger wave covers the length with fewer clamps, and Mermade describes it as the undone look. If you want a more defined, tighter pattern on the same length, the 25mm suits that aim but takes more steps.

Both listings start with hair that is clean, dry and free of tangles, divided into sections with clips. Smaller sections give more defined waves. Neither size guarantees a finish on every hair type, so start on a low setting and adjust.
