Cleaning a hair brush is mostly about removing loose hair and keeping the surface pleasant to use. The safest routine depends on its materials, especially if it has a wooden handle or a cushioned base.

Cleaning a comb instead? Use how to clean a hair comb for the teeth, spine and material checks. A brush’s cushion or bristle-cleaning method is not automatically a comb-cleaning method.

Quick answer and supplies

Remove hair first, then clean with as little moisture as the material needs. Keep a suitable maker-recommended cleaning tool, a soft cloth and a towel nearby. For heated or hot-air brushes, unplug first and never immerse the appliance.

An electrical smoothing brush needs straightening-brush care, not this ordinary-brush washing sequence. An airflow brush has its own hot-air-brush cleaning method, including model-specific outlet and filter checks.

Remove loose hair first

Lift visible loose hair with your fingers or a suitable maker-recommended tool, working gently between the contacts.

Use fingers or a suitable maker-recommended cleaning tool to lift small amounts of visible hair between the contacts. Do not drive pointed objects into a cushion or use the base as a lever. If a bundle resists, separate it into smaller accessible areas.

Doing this little step whenever hair collects makes the more thorough clean much simpler. It also lets the brush move through your hair more evenly the next time you style. Put the removed hair in the bin rather than rinsing a large amount into the sink.

Clean the surface carefully

Use a lightly damp cloth or follow the maker’s care instructions. Avoid prolonged soaking when the brush materials are not designed for it.

For a plastic brush, a soft cloth lightly dampened with warm water can be a useful first pass around the handle, back and bristle base. If the maker says it is washable, follow that guidance rather than assuming every material can be cleaned the same way. A wooden handle, natural bristles or a padded base may need much less moisture.

Work around the bristles rather than scrubbing them aggressively. Surface residue can often be lifted with a cloth or a soft, clean toothbrush used only for the brush. Check the exact instructions for joins, cushions and openings, including any permitted pad washing and drying method. Avoid cleaners that are not recommended for the finish.

Let it dry fully

Place the brush bristle-side down or in a position that lets moisture leave before the next use.

Routine and troubleshooting

Remove hair as it collects, wipe a brush when residue is visible, and do a fuller clean when the base feels crowded. Stubborn residue may need several gentle passes rather than force. Grey or white lint is usually ordinary buildup; see what grey fuzz on a hairbrush usually is. If odour remains after a suitable clean and complete drying, inspect the brush for trapped moisture or damage and follow the maker’s replacement guidance.

For material-specific context, compare boar bristle and nylon brushes. If you use a hot-air brush, read the dedicated hot-air brush cleaning guide; electrical appliances must not be immersed.

Blot away visible moisture with a clean towel, then leave the brush in an airy place rather than putting it straight into a drawer. Positioning the bristles downward can help water move away from the base, provided it does not bend them. Give the brush time to feel dry to the touch before using it again.

A simple routine is enough: remove loose hair as needed, wipe the surface when it no longer feels fresh and do a more careful clean when you notice a build-up around the bristles. The right frequency depends on how often you use the brush and the products in your styling routine.

Care for the brush you own

Check whether the brush has a wooden handle, a rubber cushion, natural bristles or a vented plastic body before choosing a cleaning method. The maker's guidance should take priority where it differs from a general routine. Gentle material care helps the brush stay pleasant to hold and keeps its bristles arranged for everyday grooming.

If a brush has bent, missing or uncomfortable bristles, cleaning may not be the only consideration. Compare how it feels through a section with a brush in good condition, then decide whether a replacement would make styling more straightforward.

Separate manual tools from powered brushes

This guide covers unpowered brushes and combs. Before adding water, identify the exact construction and read its maker's care instructions. A molded plastic detangler, a cushioned pad, natural bristles and a wooden handle may require different moisture and drying methods. Do not assume a pad is removable.
