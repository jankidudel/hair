Start by checking the cool-shot control or cool mode in your model’s manual. Some dryers change heat automatically in a particular mode. If normal airflow stays cold after choosing a documented heat setting, stop checking settings and contact the maker. Everything on this page stays with the controls and the user maintenance your manual allows; it does not involve opening the dryer.

Quick answer

Check the cool control and any automatic mode first, then select a documented heat setting. If the air stays cold on every heat setting, stop checking and contact the manufacturer. Unplug and stop using the dryer straight away if you notice an odd smell, sparking or damage.

Example: how a cool shot is meant to work

Philips’ support page for its cool shot (models HP8260/00 and HP4935/00) describes it as a burst of cold air to finish and set a style: when you have finished drying a section, press the cool shot button and direct the cool airflow at your hair. On those models, cold air while the button is pressed is the intended behaviour, not a fault. Your dryer may use a button you hold, a switch that stays on, or a cold position on the heat control; the manual says which.

Some dryers with automatic or sensor-led modes adjust heat themselves. If the cold air appears only in one such mode, that mode’s description in the manual is the place to check before assuming a fault.

When to stop using it

Electrical Safety First’s hair dryer advice says to unplug the dryer immediately and contact the retailer or manufacturer if you notice an odd smell, any sparking, or any cracks or damage. It also says not to ignore burn marks, buzzing or crackling sounds, blowing fuses, tripping circuit breakers, or a plug or socket that feels hot.

  1. Switch off and unplug the dryer.
  2. Let it cool on a heat-resistant surface.
  3. Do not open it or try internal repairs.
  4. Contact the manufacturer or retailer with the model code.

If you also see an indicator on a Dyson dryer, match the Supersonic flashing lights to your model. For a hair dryer that cuts out completely rather than running cold, use the shutdown symptom table.

Check the settings in order

  1. Find the cool control. Look for a cool-shot button, a snowflake symbol or a cold position on the heat switch, and confirm in the manual whether it is held or latched.
  2. Select a documented heat setting. Choose the heat position the manual describes, with the cool control off.
  3. Leave any automatic mode. If your model has one, switch to manual heat and speed so the dryer is not choosing for you.
  4. Give it a moment. Some dryers take a short time to reach the selected heat after switching on or changing setting; your manual may say how long.

If the air is still cold after those four steps, you have ruled out the controls. That is the point to stop and contact the manufacturer with the model code from the rating label, rather than trying further settings or cleaning parts the manual does not mention.

Separate cold air from weak air

If the air is cold and noticeably weaker, the accessible intake or filter may be blocked. Electrical Safety First also advises checking that air openings are not blocked and following the maker’s instructions for cleaning filters. Use how to clean a hair dryer filter for that check, and why your hair dryer takes so long if drying is genuinely slower.
