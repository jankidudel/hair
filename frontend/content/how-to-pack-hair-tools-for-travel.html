Packing a hair tool starts before the suitcase closes. Confirm that the appliance is suitable for the trip, plan when it can cool after its last use and leave space for its cord and the attachments you will actually need. A pouch that closes is only one part of that decision.

Quick answer

Turn off and unplug the tool as instructed, allow it to cool fully and pack it in the permitted case or cover without straining the cord. Keep the plug and loose attachments from pressing against working surfaces. Check electrical compatibility and current travel restrictions separately; a compact shape or heat-resistant bag does not answer those questions.

Choose the routine before the number of tools

Name the styles you expect to repeat and which appliance performs each necessary task. If accommodation provides a suitable dryer, verify what is available before assuming you need to carry another. A supplied dryer may not have the attachment or control you use, so availability and usefulness are separate questions.

Use travel hair-dryer selection or travel flat-iron selection when choosing the appliance. This packing sequence begins after that choice; it does not rank travel models or establish that one kit suits every trip.

Finish electrical and carriage checks before packing

Read the exact appliance's rating and manual for the destination. Plug adapters versus voltage converters owns that electrical distinction. Do not infer voltage compatibility from a folding handle, small size or the word travel.

For a flight, check the airline and relevant departure, connection and destination requirements for the exact item. Battery-powered and gas-powered models need their own checks. For UK journeys, GOV.UK's electrical-items guidance is an official starting point and directs readers to additional airline and battery information. It is not a worldwide permission list for every cordless appliance.

Give cooling its own place in the departure plan

Complete the appliance's shutdown and cooling directions before closing it into luggage. There is no shared ten-minute or thirty-minute interval that proves every tool is ready. If you style just before checkout, allow enough time for the exact tool rather than relying on the bag's lining.

Cloud Nine's UK care guidance distinguishes cooling from putting its tools away. It also describes loose cable loops and keeping the plug away from a straightener's plates. Those are named care directions, not proof that every case or guard fits every appliance.

Follow the model's guard and cable directions. Do not copy a supplied straightener guard onto a dryer or use a zipper to compress an overfilled kit. Heat mat and pouch selection owns accessory fit; a new pouch is optional when the existing case already suits the task.

Inspect the kit again on arrival

Open the case and check the working surfaces, attachments, plug and cord before use. A shifted attachment or pressure mark in a pouch is a reason to inspect the arrangement, not automatically evidence that the appliance is damaged. If the tool or cord is visibly damaged or operation is abnormal, follow the maker's service guidance rather than trying it repeatedly.

Set up a suitable resting area at the destination before switching on. Keep the packing checklist for the return trip too, especially if the final morning's routine differs from the outward journey.
